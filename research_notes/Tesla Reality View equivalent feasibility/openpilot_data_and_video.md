## Hardware: comma 3X vs comma four (SoC, GPU/NPU, RAM, cameras, spare compute, thermal limits)

### Takeaway
Both devices use a Qualcomm Snapdragon 845 with 128 GB storage and three cameras (wide road, narrow road, wide driver), but the image sensors differ: comma 3X = 3x OmniVision OX03C10 (1928x1208), comma four = 3x OmniVision OS04C10 (2688x1520 native, used at 1344x760). No published figure exists for RAM, for spare CPU/GPU while driving, or for lens field of view; these must be measured on the device.

### Inferences
- Computed horizontal field of view (pinhole, 2*atan(width/2/f)): comma 3X narrow road about 40 degrees horizontal (about 26 degrees vertical); comma 3X wide road about 119 degrees horizontal nominal. comma four narrow road about 61 degrees horizontal (about 37 degrees vertical); comma four wide road about 115 degrees horizontal nominal. The wide values are unreliable: the lens is a fisheye and the code itself doubts the focal length.
- The comma four narrow camera is much wider than the 3X narrow camera (about 61 vs 40 degrees) and has fewer pixels per degree in the frame openpilot uses (1344 px over 61 degrees is about 22 px/degree; 1928 px over 40 degrees is about 48 px/degree). Distant-object detail for any add-on detector is therefore worse on the comma four.
- The Snapdragon 845 has an Adreno 630 GPU and a Hexagon DSP but no modern NPU. openpilot's own models run through tinygrad on the Qualcomm GPU (see model section). A second heavy network on the same GPU competes directly with the driving model.
- "Zero throttling" is a marketing statement. The thermal bands in code show the comma four is allowed to run about 4 degrees C hotter than the 3X before each status change.
- The external GPU path (chestnut) is the only vendor-supported way to add large compute. It is designed for the driving model, but shows that USB3-attached GPUs work with tinygrad on this device.

### Gaps
- RAM size: no source read states it for either device (shop, blog and hardware repository all omit it). Measure with `free -m` on the device.
- Spare CPU and GPU while openpilot drives: no published number found. The `procLog` (0.5 Hz) and `deviceState` (2 Hz) services give per-process CPU, memory and temperature and should be read from a real drive.
- Lens field-of-view specifications from comma: not published in the pages read. Only the code focal lengths are available.
- Sensor HDR for OS04C10 (comma four): not stated by comma in the pages read. The 140 dB figure applies to the OX03C10 of the 3X.
- What "845 MAX" means technically (overclock, cooling, or branding) is not explained in the pages read.
- Whether the 20 Hz camera rate can be raised: not investigated.

## Model outputs: what modelV2 publishes, what it does not, architecture and model history, latents, comma research artifacts

### Takeaway
The driving model publishes a sparse, driving-oriented description: ego plan, up to 4 lane lines, 2 road edges, up to 3 lead hypotheses with probabilities, pose, desire and disengage predictions. It publishes no general object list, no bounding boxes, no depth, no segmentation and no occupancy. The 2025-2026 models are end-to-end policies trained inside a learned world-model simulator; the world model itself does not run on the device.

### Cited Findings
- `ModelDataV2` fields: `frameId`, `frameIdExtra`, `frameAge`, `frameDropPerc`, `timestampEof`, `modelExecutionTime`, `rawPredictions` (Data), `big` (Bool), `position`/`orientation`/`velocity`/`orientationRate`/`acceleration` (XYZT trajectories), `laneLines` (list of XYZT) with `laneLineProbs` and `laneLineStds`, `roadEdges` with `roadEdgeStds`, `leadsV3`, `meta`, `confidence` (red/yellow/green), `action` (`desiredCurvature`, `desiredAcceleration`, `shouldStop`). There is no field for objects, classes, boxes, depth, segmentation or occupancy (local checkout) — [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- `LeadDataV3`: `prob` ("probability that car is your lead at time t"), `probTime`, and lists `t`, `x`, `y`, `v`, `a` with standard deviations; x and y are relative position in device frame. There is no lead width, height, class or heading (local checkout) — [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- `MetaData`: `engagedProb`, `desirePrediction`, `desireState`, `disengagePredictions` (brake/gas/steer-override probabilities and brake 3/4/5 m/s2 probabilities over time), `hardBrakePredicted`, `laneChangeState`, `laneChangeDirection` (local checkout) — [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- Output geometry constants: 33 trajectory points (`IDX_N = 33`) on a quadratic spacing out to 10 s (`T_IDXS`) and 192 m (`X_IDXS`); `NUM_LANE_LINES = 4`, `NUM_ROAD_EDGES = 2`; lead times `LEAD_T_IDXS = [0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10]` s with lead offsets `[0, 2, 4]` s; `MODEL_RUN_FREQ = 20`, `MODEL_CONTEXT_FREQ = 5`; `FEATURE_LEN = 512`; `DESIRE_LEN = 8` (local checkout) — [selfdrive/modeld/constants.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/modeld/constants.py)
- Message rates: `modelV2` 20 Hz, `drivingModelData` 20 Hz (a compact message with action, lane-line meta and polynomial path), `cameraOdometry` 20 Hz (device-frame translation and rotation rates with standard deviations, `wideFromDeviceEuler`, `roadTransformTrans`) (local checkout) — [cereal/services.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/services.py)
- Latents and raw output: modeld copies the model's `hidden_state` output slice back into the `prev_feat` input each run, and when the environment variable `SEND_RAW_PRED` is set it puts the whole raw output vector into `modelV2.rawPredictions`. The model is loaded as a compiled tinygrad pickle with `input_shapes` and `output_slices` metadata (local checkout) — [selfdrive/modeld/modeld.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/modeld/modeld.py)
- Model files in the checkout: `driving_supercombo.onnx` (58.1 MB), `big_driving_supercombo.onnx` (730.5 MB), `dmonitoring_model.onnx` (7.2 MB), compiled `driving_tinygrad.pkl` chunks. The models README says ONNX architectures can be viewed in netron (local checkout) — [selfdrive/modeld/models](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/tree/master/selfdrive/modeld/models)
- Model history from release notes: 0.10.0 (2025-08-05) new training architecture "Described in our CVPR paper: Learning to Drive from a World Model", "Longitudinal MPC replaced by E2E planning from World Model in Experimental Mode"; 0.10.1 (2025-09-08) world model with removed global localization inputs, 2x parameters, 4x segments, new "VAE Compression Model", "Driving Vision Model: trained on 4x the number of segments"; 0.10.3 (2025-12-17) "New temporal policy architecture"; 0.11.0 (2026-03-17) "Fully trained using a learned simulator"; 0.11.2 (2026-08-12 in RELEASES.md) "Big model with 880M parameters", "Support for big models running on an external GPU" — [openpilot RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.11 architecture (blog, 2026-03-17, "WMI model", PR #36798): a world model is trained on unlabeled fleet data, then a small policy is trained on its interactions with the world model. Frame compressor is a ViT (50M-parameter encoder, 100M decoder) that encodes narrow and wide views of 3x128x256 each into a 32x16x32 latent. The world model is a 2B-parameter block-causal diffusion transformer (48 layers, 25 heads, width 1600) trained with Rectified Flow on 2.5M minutes of video, 5 fps, 2 s past context, 0-7 s simulation window, 15 Euler steps, 12.2 frames/s per GPU — [comma blog, openpilot 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)
- Key statement for visualization (0.11 blog): the on-device policy is split. An "off-policy" FastViT predicts "lane lines, road edges, lead car information, etc."; these outputs are used for visualization and, for lead cars, as a fallback, and are not part of the end-to-end policy. The "on-policy" model is a small transformer with 2 s of context at 5 fps — [comma blog, openpilot 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)
- Paper: "Learning to Drive from a World Model", arXiv 2504.19077 (April 2025), CVPR 2025 Workshops pages 1964-1973. It trains an end-to-end policy in two simulators (a reprojective novel-view-synthesis simulator and a learned world model) and deploys it in a real driver-assistance system — [arXiv 2504.19077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19077), [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/DDADS/html/Goff_Learning_to_Drive_from_a_World_Model_CVPRW_2025_paper.html)
- The world model is a training-time simulator and is not run at inference on the device (secondary source, consistent with the 0.11 blog's GPU sampling speed) — [kvynlim industry notes](https://kvynlim.github.io/industry-research/80-industry-intel/companies/comma-ai/production-world-model)
- Runtime: comma moved the on-device stack to tinygrad in 0.9.8, for comma 3X and PC — [comma blog, 0.9.8](https://blog.comma.ai/098release/)
- 0.11.2 big model (blog summary): ConvNeXt-XL vision backbone plus temporal, residual and point policies; about 93.5 GFLOPs versus 1.7 GFLOPs for the 0.11.1 small model; plotted outputs are plan, action, desire_pred and pose — [comma blog, 0.11.2](https://blog.comma.ai/0112release/)
- Conflict on the big model: RELEASES.md says "880M parameters" and dates 0.11.2 as 2026-08-12; the summary of the 0.11.2 blog post gave about 382.9M total parameters and a post date of 2026-10-23 — [openpilot RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md); contradicted by [comma blog, 0.11.2](https://blog.comma.ai/0112release/)
- commaVQ: 100,000 one-minute driving segments at 20 fps, each frame compressed by a VQ-VAE into 128 tokens of 10 bits; the repository includes a GPT world model trained on 3,000,000 minutes that predicts next tokens ("imagine future frames"). The Hugging Face dataset card lists the MIT licence — [commaai/commavq](https://github.com/commaai/commavq), [Hugging Face dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/commaai/commavq/blob/main/README.md)
- comma10k: 10,000 crowd-labelled segmentation images from comma dash cameras; MIT licensed; 5 labels in 3 broad classes (an academic paper lists them as road, lane markings, undrivable, movable, my car) — [comma blog, crowdsourced segnet](https://blog.comma.ai/crowdsourced-segnet-you-can-help/), [commaai/comma10k](https://www.github.com/commaai/comma10k), [arXiv 2004.06320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.06320v1.pdf)
- Licences: openpilot is MIT licensed — [commaai/openpilot](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot). sunnypilot in this checkout ships a "Custom MIT License" (Haibin Wen, SUNNYPILOT LLC) that requires explicit written permission for commercial, for-profit or closed-source use and requires a visible acknowledgment — [sunnypilot LICENSE.md](https://github.com/sunnypilot/sunnypilot/blob/master/LICENSE.md) (text read from the local checkout's `LICENSE.md`)

### Inferences
- A Tesla-style scene with many surrounding vehicles, pedestrians, cones and traffic lights cannot be built from `modelV2` alone. The model gives the road shape (lanes, edges, path) and at most the lead vehicle hypotheses in the ego lane region. Everything else needs another source: radar tracks, the car's own ADAS bus, or an extra perception network.
- Lane lines and road edges are an auxiliary ("off-policy") head kept largely for visualization. comma has no product reason to keep them accurate, and a future model could change or drop them. A product built on them carries that risk; pin a model version.
- The lane/edge geometry is rich enough for a 3D road ribbon: 4 lines and 2 edges, each 33 points to 192 m with x/y/z and per-line probability or standard deviation, at 20 Hz.
- Latent tapping is technically possible (the 512-wide feature and `hidden_state` slice, `SEND_RAW_PRED`), but the latents have no documented meaning, change with every model release, and comma publishes no decoder for them. Treat this as research, not a planning basis.
- The world-model frame compressor (128x256 inputs, 32x16x32 latent) and the diffusion world model are training-side and too large for the Snapdragon 845 (2B parameters, 12.2 frames/s on a data-centre GPU). They are not a route to on-device rendering.
- comma10k is the only comma artifact directly useful for an on-device add-on: an MIT dataset to train a small 5-class segmenter (road, lane markings, movable objects). It gives image-space masks, not 3D boxes.

### Gaps
- No comma depth model or depth dataset was found in the sources read. Not confirmed absent; only not found.
- The exact tensor list of the current ONNX (`driving_supercombo.onnx`) was not opened. Inspect it with netron or `get_model_metadata.py` to list every output slice, including any not copied into `modelV2`.
- Whether the comma four and comma 3X run the same model input resolution on device was not confirmed.
- The big-model parameter count and the 0.11.2 date conflict (above) is unresolved.
- commaVQ licence: MIT per the Hugging Face card; a LICENSE file in the GitHub repository was not confirmed.
- Whether comma10k reached all 10,000 published images was not confirmed (the old blog post mentions 1k released at that time).
- Model execution time on comma four and 3X: no published number found. It is logged per frame in `modelV2.modelExecutionTime`.

## Video: getting live camera frames off the device (VisionIPC, encoderd, webrtcd, livestream services, comma connect/athena), resolution, fps, latency, CPU cost

### Takeaway
There are three practical paths: raw frames over VisionIPC (on-device only), the full-resolution H.265 logging streams over the cereal bridge, and a dedicated low-latency "livestream" H.264 encoder at 1280x720 (comma four) or 1152x720 (comma 3X), 20 fps, 5 Mbit/s default, that feeds `webrtcd`. Stock openpilot runs the livestream path only when parked or on a non-car; forks (dragonpilot, opview's patch, and this local fork's web HUD) enable it while driving. No measured latency or CPU cost was found in public sources.

### Cited Findings
- Process conditions (local checkout): `camerad` and `encoderd` run onroad; `stream_encoderd` (`./encoderd --stream`) and `webrtcd` run when `IsLiveStreaming` is set or on a non-car; the cereal `bridge` runs only on a non-car. In this fork, `stream_encoderd` and `camerad` also run when the fork's own `WebHudVideo` param is set — [system/manager/process_config.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/manager/process_config.py)
- Logging encoders (local checkout): `narrowRoadEncodeData` -> `fcamera.hevc`, `wideRoadEncodeData` -> `ecamera.hevc`, `cabinEncodeData` -> `dcamera.hevc`; bitrate 5 Mbit/s with GOP 20 when input width is 1344 or less (comma four), else 10 Mbit/s with GOP 30 (comma 3X); no B-frames. `qNarrowRoadEncodeData` -> `qcamera.ts` is H.264 at 526x330, 256 kbit/s — [system/loggerd/loggerd.h](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/loggerd/loggerd.h)
- Livestream encoders (local checkout): `livestreamNarrowRoadEncodeData`, `livestreamWideRoadEncodeData`, `livestreamCabinEncodeData`; H.264; frame size 1152x720 on TIZI and 1280x720 on MICI; default bitrate 5,000,000 bit/s, overridable with the `STREAM_BITRATE` environment variable; GOP 5 (a keyframe every 5 frames, 0.25 s at 20 fps); not recorded — [system/loggerd/loggerd.h](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/loggerd/loggerd.h)
- All encode services are declared at 20 Hz (local checkout) — [cereal/services.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/services.py)
- webrtcd (local checkout): a stream session takes a list of cameras (`driver`, `wideRoad`, `road`, mapped to the three livestream encode services), builds a WebRTC answer with the `teleoprtc` library, forwards the already-encoded H.264 frames, and can bridge cereal services in both directions over a data channel as JSON (`{"type", "logMonoTime", "valid", "data"}`), polled every 10 ms. It includes a `LivestreamBitrateController` driven by receiver reports, supports camera switching and keyframe requests, and clears `IsLiveStreaming` 5 s after the last session ends — [system/webrtc/webrtcd.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/webrtc/webrtcd.py), [system/webrtc/device/video.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/webrtc/device/video.py)
- webrtcd is reached with an SDP exchange via `POST /stream` on port 5001 (as used by a third-party client) — [eFiniLan/opview](https://github.com/eFiniLan/opview)
- comma connect / athena (local checkout): `startStream(sdp, enabled)` is an athena RPC; when offroad it sets `IsLiveStreaming` and waits for webrtcd; it requests camera `["wideRoad"]` and outgoing services `["carState", "deviceState"]`; the comment says webrtcd is "shut off on ignition" — [system/athena/athenad.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/athena/athenad.py)
- 0.11.2 release notes: "Live stream cameras from comma connect", "Generate dashcam clips from comma connect", "Remote comma body control from comma connect". The blog says live streaming shows road and driver cameras, needs an internet connection (home Wi-Fi or comma prime LTE) and a comma power unit, and is used while the car is parked — [openpilot RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md), [comma blog, 0.11.2](https://blog.comma.ai/0112release/)
- Full-quality LAN path (tools/camerastream): on the device run the cereal `bridge`, `camerad` and `encoderd`; on a PC `compressed_vipc.py <ip>` decodes the streams and republishes them over VisionIPC, and `watch3.py` displays all three cameras. Device and PC must be on the same openpilot commit (local checkout) — [tools/camerastream README](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/tools/camerastream/README.md)
- VisionIPC: modeld receives camera frames through `VisionIpcClient` / `VisionBuf` (NV12 buffers) from camerad, the on-device zero-copy frame path (local checkout) — [selfdrive/modeld/modeld.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/modeld/modeld.py)
- opview enables onroad streaming on stock openpilot by changing the `webrtcd` and `stream_encoderd` conditions from `notcar` to `or_(notcar, only_onroad)` in `process_config.py`, and by wrapping webrtcd's audio initialisation in a try/except because of an ALSA "Device unavailable" error; alternatively it says to use dragonpilot 0.10.3 or later. It prefers H.264 through SDP rewriting and gets telemetry on the WebRTC data channel — [eFiniLan/opview](https://github.com/eFiniLan/opview)
- This local fork already ships an onroad video path: `sunnypilot/webhud/video.py` passes the livestream encoder's H.264 (1280x720, keyframe every 5 frames) untouched over a WebSocket `/video` with a per-frame header (keyframe flag, camera index, `frameId`, `timestampEof`), plus JSON `calib` (per-camera width, height, K and projection matrix M from car frame to stream pixels), `model` (path, lanes, lane probabilities, edges, leads, matched by frame id) and `hud` messages at about 20 Hz. The page decodes with WebCodecs. `WebHudVideo` is set while a viewer is connected and cleared 10 s after the last one leaves — local file `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/video.py` and `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/README.md`; public counterpart project [moistpope/oceanfront-view](https://github.com/moistpope/oceanfront-view)
- UI-screen streaming alternative (op-remote-ui): grabs the Qt main window every 100 ms (10 fps), JPEG at 85 % quality, shared memory then WebSocket on port 8081; the README claims typical latency 100-300 ms, CPU "<5% on typical hardware" and 500 KB/s to 2 MB/s, without measurement method — [ecpp/op-remote-ui](https://github.com/ecpp/op-remote-ui)
- CommaView (third-party Android app, beta) reports Live View at about 20 fps over the local network with no cloud in the video path, using its own device runtime `commaviewd` — [CommaView](https://commaview.com/)

### Inferences
- The best ready-made source for an external "reality view" screen is the livestream encoder: hardware H.264, 720p, 20 fps, short GOP for fast join, already frame-id stamped so overlays can be matched to frames. A second hardware encode session per camera is what it costs; the Snapdragon 845 encoder already handles three H.265 logging streams plus qcamera at the same time while driving.
- At the default 5 Mbit/s the stream fits easily in a local Wi-Fi link (device hotspot or shared network). Latency is bounded below by 50 ms frame time plus encode, network and decode; the only published figure near this (100-300 ms) is for a different JPEG method and is an unverified README claim.
- The H.265 logging streams are full resolution (1928x1208 on 3X, 1344x760 on comma four) but need the cereal bridge and have a long GOP (20 or 30 frames), so they are poor for live joining and add 1-1.5 s recovery after a lost frame.
- For a rendered 3D view (not a camera view), video is optional. Only the low-rate structured messages are needed, which cost far less bandwidth and no encoder.
- comma connect livestream is offroad-only by design and goes through the cloud signalling path; it is not a path for an in-car display while driving.
- A second consumer of raw frames on the device (an added detector) should attach to VisionIPC as modeld does; this avoids any decode cost.

### Gaps
- Measured end-to-end latency over Wi-Fi for the livestream H.264 path: not found in any public source.
- Measured CPU cost of `stream_encoderd` and `webrtcd` while driving: not found. Measure with `procLog` on the device.
- Whether upstream openpilot intends to allow livestream while onroad: no statement found.
- Whether more than one livestream camera can be encoded at the same time without frame drops while driving: not found.
- CommaView's protocol, codec, resolution and latency are not disclosed on its page.

## Other data: calibration (camera extrinsics), pose, GPS, radar leads and tracks, carState; cereal message rates

### Takeaway
Everything needed to place model outputs in a car-centred 3D frame is published: extrinsic calibration at 4 Hz, device motion and camera odometry at 20 Hz, GPS at 10 Hz, fused leads at 20 Hz and raw radar points at 20 Hz. Note that in 2026 upstream renamed the "live*" services (for example `liveCalibration` became `extrinsicsCalibration`), so older documentation and client code use different names.

### Cited Findings
- Service rates (local checkout, Hz): `modelV2` 20, `drivingModelData` 20, `cameraOdometry` 20, `radarState` 20, `radarTracks` 20, `extrinsicsCalibration` 4, `deviceMotion` 20, `vehicleParameters` 20, `liveLocationKalman` 20 (listed under sunnypilot services), `gpsLocationExternal` 10, `gpsLocation` 1, `carState` 100, `carControl` 100, `controlsState` 100, `selfdriveState` 100, `longitudinalPlan` 20, `can` 100, `gyroscope` 104, `accelerometer` 104, `deviceState` 2, `procLog` 0.5, `driverStateV2` 20, `driverMonitoringState` 20, camera state services 20, `thumbnail` 1/60 — [cereal/services.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/services.py)
- Renames: the local git history contains the upstream commit "Rename "live" cereal services (#38601)". In the checkout the schema uses `extrinsicsCalibration`, `deviceMotion`, `vehicleParameters`, `radarTracks`, `narrowRoadCameraState`, `cabinCameraState`, `narrowRoadEncodeData`; `liveTracksDEPRECATED` remains only as a deprecated field — [openpilot PR #38601](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/pull/38601), [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- `ExtrinsicsCalibration`: `calStatus` (uncalibrated, calibrated, invalid, recalibrating), `calPerc`, `validBlocks`, `extrinsicMatrix` ("view_frame_from_road_frame"), `rpyCalib` (direction-of-travel vector in device frame), `rpyCalibSpread`, `wideFromDeviceEuler`, `height` (local checkout) — [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- `DeviceMotion`: `orientationNED`, `velocityDevice`, `accelerationDevice`, `angularVelocityDevice` (each x/y/z with standard deviations and a valid flag), `inputsOK`, `posenetOK`, `sensorsOK`, `timestamp` (local checkout) — [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- `RadarState`: only `leadOne` and `leadTwo`, each with `dRel` (m from front bumper), `yRel` (m, left positive), `vRel`, `vLead`, `vLeadK`, `aLeadK`, `present`, `modelProb` (vision model lead probability), `radar` (true if radar-matched, false if vision-only), `radarTrackId` (local checkout) — [cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)
- `radarTracks` carries `Car.RadarData`: a list of `RadarPoint` with `trackId` (no reuse), `dRel`, `yRel`, `vRel`; acceleration, lateral velocity and the measured flag are deprecated. There is no object size or class (local checkout) — [opendbc car.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/opendbc/blob/master/opendbc/car/car.capnp)
- The stock UI subscribes to `modelV2`, `extrinsicsCalibration`, `radarState`, `carState`, `carParams`, `selfdriveState`, `longitudinalPlan`, `gpsLocationExternal`, `driverMonitoringState`, `driverStateV2`, camera states and others (local checkout) — [selfdrive/ui/ui_state.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/ui/ui_state.py)
- Remote access to these messages: webrtcd's outgoing data-channel bridge sends any requested service as JSON; the cereal `bridge` process republishes msgq services over the network for PC tools (stock: non-car only) (local checkout) — [system/webrtc/webrtcd.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/webrtc/webrtcd.py), [system/manager/process_config.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/system/manager/process_config.py)

### Inferences
- `radarTracks` is the only stock source of multiple surrounding objects, and only on cars whose radar openpilot parses. Points have position and closing speed only, so a renderer must invent size, heading and class, and must filter ghosts and stationary clutter itself.
- Vision-only cars get at most the model's lead hypotheses: effectively one or two vehicles ahead. Adjacent-lane traffic is not available from openpilot without added perception or the car's own ADAS/blind-spot data.
- `extrinsicMatrix`, `rpyCalib`, `height` and the intrinsics table give a complete camera model for projecting between image and road frame, which is how the stock UI and third-party clients draw overlays.
- 20 Hz structured data needs interpolation to render smoothly at 60 fps; `carState` (100 Hz) and `deviceMotion` can drive ego-motion compensation between model frames.
- Client code must handle both the old and new service names if it is to work across openpilot versions and forks that have not merged the 2026 rename.

### Gaps
- Which cars expose useful `radarTracks` (and how many points) was not surveyed; it is car-port specific.
- The exact fields of `gpsLocationExternal` and `liveLocationKalman` were not read.
- JSON size and CPU cost of bridging `modelV2` at 20 Hz through webrtcd's Python proxy were not found; the proxy is a blocking poll in an async loop every 10 ms.

## Existing visualizations and forks: what they achieved and where they stopped

### Takeaway
Every public project found reproduces or relocates openpilot's 2D augmented camera view (camera image plus path, lanes, edges and a lead marker); none found renders a Tesla-like 3D scene from openpilot's own outputs. The one 3D "reality view" located (Oceanscape / Oceanfront View for the Fisker Ocean, with this fork's web HUD bridge) gets its surrounding cars, signs and lights from the car's own ADAS and radar CAN buses, not from the comma model.

### Cited Findings
- Stock openpilot UI: the onroad screen is an augmented road view with a model renderer (path, lane lines, road edges, lead indicators), HUD renderer, alert renderer, driver state, confidence ball and torque bar; there is a separate `mici` (comma four) variant of the onroad UI. Lead indicators come from `radarState.leadOne/leadTwo` and are drawn only when openpilot longitudinal control is active (local checkout) — [selfdrive/ui/onroad](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/tree/master/selfdrive/ui/onroad), [model_renderer.py](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/ui/onroad/model_renderer.py)
- opview (eFiniLan): Flutter app for Android and iOS that clones the stock onroad UI on a phone or Android head unit (minSdk 21). Video and telemetry over WebRTC from `webrtcd`; components mirror `augmented_road_view`, `model_renderer`, `hud_renderer`, `alert_renderer`; "No 3D library needed", projection is 3x3 matrix math in Dart. Limits: same local network, iOS not test-built, Android Auto and CarPlay not possible (app categories and template UIs), needs the process_config and audio patches on stock openpilot. 22 stars, 49 commits, copyright 2026 — [eFiniLan/opview](https://github.com/eFiniLan/opview)
- CommaView: unofficial, invite-only beta Android app (app 0.0.166-beta) for phones, tablets and head units; shows the road camera with the fork's overlay or its own (speed, MAX, speed limit, car ahead, lane changes), about 20 fps, recording, RTMP/YouTube streaming at 720p or 1080p, picture-in-picture. Installs a runtime `commaviewd` over SSH and "a small hook to openpilot's on-screen UI". Supports comma 3X and comma four (not comma 3) and openpilot, sunnypilot, StarPilot, dragonpilot, carrotpilot, BluePilot, OPGM and others. No 3D view; no licence stated — [CommaView](https://commaview.com/)
- T-UI (mlocoteta): Android head-unit/tablet display for StarPilot showing "lane lines, path, lead car, other cars", engagement, set speed, speed limit, alerts and a vector map. Read-only HTTP polling of `GET /api/hud` (about 4 Hz) and `GET /api/hud/scene` (NDJSON at 10 Hz) served by a separate patch on StarPilot's Galaxy web server. Rendering is a Java core with an Android Canvas adapter (2D). Status "v0.3 in development", "Not tested on a device yet", 0 stars, 5 commits, Apache-2.0 — [mlocoteta/t-ui](https://github.com/mlocoteta/t-ui)
- op-remote-ui (ecpp): streams the device's own Qt UI to a browser as JPEG at 10 fps with touch injection; intended for debugging; no licence stated; 10 stars — [ecpp/op-remote-ui](https://github.com/ecpp/op-remote-ui)
- StarPilot (firestar5683): fork built on FrogPilot, formerly GM-targeted; supports comma 3, 3X and 4. "Galaxy" is its phone portal/app (Wi-Fi, HTTPS link or Bluetooth) to change settings, download models, update, run diagnostics and "visualize live model outputs for tuning"; also an on-device UI replay over recorded footage. 167 stars — [firestar5683/StarPilot](https://github.com/firestar5683/StarPilot)
- FrogPilot: onroad UI additions include a blind-spot path drawn when a vehicle is detected in the blind spot, an acceleration path, adjustable lane-line/path/road-edge widths, and screen-border colours for blind spot and turn signal. Supports comma 3, 3X and four — [FrogAi/FrogPilot](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot), [FrogPilot releases](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/releases), [DeepWiki feature summary](https://deepwiki.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/1.1-frogpilot-features-and-enhancements)
- dragonpilot: opview names dragonpilot 0.10.3 or later as a fork that works with it without patches (i.e. serves the WebRTC stream while driving) — [eFiniLan/opview](https://github.com/eFiniLan/opview)
- Oceanscape (formerly Oceanfront View; moistpope, crediting BenKayUK's OceanScape fork): "a Tesla-style car view for the Fisker Ocean's center screen": a 3D car model with the lanes, cars, signs and lights the car's ADAS sees, the road from OpenStreetMap, car state and controls, music and navigation. three.js r160, plain ES modules, no build step; CAN decoding, sensor fusion, pose estimation and map matching in a web worker. Data from the car's own buses via an add-on board, optionally plus a comma running sunnypilot, which adds the full ADAS bus, the radar's private bus and openpilot services at 20 Hz. Served by an Android app on the car's screen or `serve.py` on a PC. The comma camera feed can appear in a window over the 3D view. 207 commits, 2 stars — [moistpope/oceanfront-view](https://github.com/moistpope/oceanfront-view)
- The matching device-side bridge exists in the local checkout: `sunnypilot/webhud` streams allow-listed raw CAN frames (ADAS bus and the radar's private bus on panda bus 1) plus compact extracts of openpilot services in 20 Hz ticks over WebSocket `/ws` on port 8088, serves OSM road tiles for map matching, publishes `sunnypilot.local` over mDNS, and is niced and idle with no viewer — local file `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/README.md`
- comma tooling: `tools/replay` replays all logged messages of a route; PlotJuggler has comma-written plugins to parse openpilot logs; `LogReader` and `Route` classes read logs. The local checkout's `tools/` also contains `cabana`, `camerastream`, `clip`, `jotpluggler`, `joystick`, `sim` — [docs.comma.ai replay](https://docs.comma.ai/tools/replay/README.html), [docs.comma.ai PlotJuggler](https://docs.comma.ai/tools/plotjuggler/README.html), [openpilot tools](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/tree/master/tools)
- Rerun is a general open-source SDK and viewer for multimodal sensor data; the search found no ready-made openpilot importer for Rerun or Foxglove — [rerun-io/rerun](https://www.github.com/rerun-io/rerun)

### Inferences
- The community stopped at "move the comma screen to a bigger display". The repeated pattern is: H.264 road camera over WebRTC or WebSocket, model geometry as JSON, 2D projection with the calibration matrix. The 3D step was not taken because openpilot provides nothing to populate a 3D scene beyond the road ribbon and one or two leads.
- The transport and overlay problems are solved several times over (opview, CommaView, this fork's web HUD). A new project should reuse one of these paths and put its effort into perception and scene construction.
- The Fisker-specific Oceanscape plus web HUD bridge is the closest existing system to the goal and is already in this repository. Its surrounding objects come from the vehicle's OEM ADAS and radar buses. A comma-only "reality view" would have to replace that source.
- Head units impose limits: Android Auto and CarPlay cannot host such an app (opview's finding), so the target must be a native Android head unit, a tablet, or a browser.
- `tools/replay` plus recorded routes gives a full offline development loop for a renderer (all messages and video replayed at real time) without a car.

### Gaps
- No public project was found that renders a Tesla-like 3D scene from openpilot outputs alone. This is "not found in the searches run", not proof that none exists; Discord, Reddit r/Comma_ai and YouTube were not searched directly (search results did not surface them).
- sunnypilot's own onroad UI differences from stock were not surveyed beyond the directory listing (`selfdrive/ui/sunnypilot`).
- dragonpilot's UI features were not read from its own repository.
- No "openpilot onroad UI 3D" experiment by comma was found.
- StarPilot's "live model outputs" visualisation was not inspected; its form (2D plot or camera overlay) is unknown.
- Licences of opview, CommaView, op-remote-ui and Oceanscape were not identified from the pages read.
- Last-commit dates were not available for most repositories.

## Community work on extra cameras, object detection (YOLO on device) or depth models on comma hardware, with measured performance

### Takeaway
No public, measured example of YOLO-class object detection or monocular depth running on a comma 3X or comma four alongside openpilot was found. The only measured add-on GPU work located is a fork's camera reprojection stage (about 4 ms on the Qualcomm GPU) and comma's own external-GPU ("chestnut") support for a much larger driving model.

### Cited Findings
- An extended web search for YOLO or object detection on comma 3X / Snapdragon 845 with openpilot returned no benchmark and no fork doing it — search run 2026-11-23, no supporting source; nearest results were general fork pages such as [FrogAi/FrogPilot](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot)
- "reprojectd" PR series in a personal fork: a daemon subscribes to camerad's narrow and wide frames, pairs them by start of frame and reprojects each pair on the Qualcomm GPU so comma 3X cameras look like comma four frames; reported "model 37.0 ms p50, stage 4.3 ms p50, no dropped frames" on a 3X with chestnut. An early PR states the manager does not start it yet — [AmyJeanes/openpilot-upstream PR #8](https://github.com/AmyJeanes/openpilot-upstream/pull/8), [PR #21](https://github.com/AmyJeanes/openpilot-upstream/pull/21)
- Compute scale reference from comma: the 0.11.1 small driving model is about 1.7 GFLOPs; the 0.11.2 big model is about 93.5 GFLOPs and needs an external GPU — [comma blog, 0.11.2](https://blog.comma.ai/0112release/)
- Driver monitoring is pinned to CPU in the checkout's tinygrad device map (`{"openpilot.selfdrive.modeld.dmonitoringmodeld": {"default": {"DEV": "CPU"}}}`) (local checkout) — [selfdrive/modeld/models](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/tree/master/selfdrive/modeld/models)
- Informal community comments describe the device as having limited compute for wider-view models — [F150 forum, Bluepilot thread](https://www.f150gen14.com/forum/threads/introducing-bluepilot-a-ford-specific-fork-for-comma3x-openpilot.24241/page-2)
- A community training pipeline for openpilot-style end-to-end models exists (work in progress), relevant only as a starting point for training custom heads — [mbalesni/openpilot-pipeline](https://github.com/mbalesni/openpilot-pipeline)
- User-supplied operational note for this specific device (not a public source): running about 10 analysis jobs on the comma exhausted RAM and killed modeld mid-drive; the owner's rule is at most 1-2 jobs, pinned to cores 0-1 and niced — local memory file `/Users/hiremanirmss/.claude/projects/-Users-hiremanirmss-Fisker-sunnypilot/memory/MEMORY.md`

### Inferences
- The on-device driving model is tiny (about 1.7 GFLOPs at 20 Hz, about 34 GFLOPs/s). A typical small detector (YOLO-nano class, several GFLOPs per frame) at 10-20 Hz would be a load of the same order as or larger than the driving model on the same Adreno GPU. That is a direct safety-relevant contention risk and must be measured before any design commitment.
- Driver monitoring being pinned to CPU suggests comma keeps the GPU reserved for the driving model, which is a further sign that GPU headroom is not generous.
- The safer architectures are off-device: send the 720p H.264 stream to the head unit or a separate computer and run detection or depth there, or use the vendor-supported external GPU path. Both keep the comma's thermal and real-time budget untouched.
- No evidence was found of anyone adding extra cameras (side or rear) to a comma device; the device has three fixed cameras, and only the two road-facing ones see traffic. A 360-degree scene is therefore not achievable from the comma alone, whatever the "360° vision" marketing wording suggests (the third camera faces the cabin).

### Gaps
- Measured FPS, latency, GPU load and temperature of any detector or depth network on a Snapdragon 845 under openpilot: not found. This needs a bench test on the device (offroad first).
- Whether tinygrad's Qualcomm backend can time-slice two networks without frame drops in modeld: not found.
- Any community project adding USB or network cameras to openpilot: not found in the searches run.
- comma Discord content could not be searched; it is the most likely place for unpublished experiments of this kind.
