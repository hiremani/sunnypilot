## 1. How does openpilot Experimental mode decide to stop, and what does comma say about reliability through 2026? Newer architectures?

### Takeaway
Stopping in Experimental mode is not a detection: it is whatever acceleration the end-to-end policy outputs, and `action.shouldStop` is only a standstill-hold flag (`v_ego < 0.3 m/s and desired accel < 0.1 m/s^2`), so it cannot be true while the car is still moving. comma has published no stop-sign/traffic-light reliability numbers; its statements run from "alpha, frequent mistakes are expected" (2022) to "users on nightly now prefer Experimental mode" (0.11, March 2026), with the architecture changing twice (world-model supervision in 0.10, policy trained entirely in a learned simulator in 0.11).

### Inferences
- Because `shouldStop` requires `v_ego < 0.3 m/s`, it is a "hold at standstill" signal. For a driver who rolls through at ~2.4 mph (1.07 m/s) it is false by construction. It is not usable as an approach-phase confirmation.
- In this fork's Stop Sign Assist the second test (`min(modelV2.velocity.x) < 1.5 m/s`) is the only one that can fire before standstill. That it did not fire on a 1.07 m/s roll-through is surprising on its face (the plan's first point should be near ego speed) and is worth checking in the log directly: plot `velocity.x[0]`, `min(velocity.x)` and `vEgo` for the 10 s around the sign, and check whether the sign had already been "finished"/passed in map terms when ego speed went under 1.5 m/s.
- With the driver driving (stock ACC, shadow mode), the model output is a prediction of human behaviour from video; it tends to follow the observed deceleration rather than lead it. Expect the model to "confirm" late relative to an engaged Experimental-mode run.
- The 0.10/0.11 changes mean thresholds tuned on pre-0.10 (MPC-era) models do not transfer; any threshold must be re-tuned per model generation, including sunnypilot's selectable models (`sunnypilot/modeld_v2`).

## 2. What signals do FrogPilot/StarPilot and sunnypilot use to detect "stop ahead" from the model, with thresholds and filtering?

### Takeaway
Both forks infer "the model wants to stop" from the shape of the planned trajectory, not from any sign/light classifier: sunnypilot DEC compares the plan's end-point distance (`position.x[32]`) with a speed-dependent expected distance; FrogPilot CEM checks the planned velocity (min planned speed <= 70% of current speed, or planned speed < 1.0 m/s at 0.5-1.5 s) through a 1.5 s first-order filter, and suppresses it when a lead is relevant. Both use filtering plus hysteresis and a lead-vehicle exclusion as the main false-positive defence.

### Cited Findings
**sunnypilot Dynamic Experimental Control (DEC)**
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** DEC only chooses between `'acc'` and `'blended'` (e2e) planning; `is_e2e` is `experimentalMode and dec.mode() == "blended"` when DEC is active. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/longitudinal_planner.py` lines 52-57
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** Slow-down detection (`_calculate_slow_down`): takes `endpoint_x = md.position.x[32]`; expected distance is interpolated from `SLOW_DOWN_BP = [0,10,20,30,40,50,55,60]` km/h to `SLOW_DOWN_DIST = [32,46,64,86,108,130,145,165]` m; if the endpoint is shorter, `urgency = min(1, 2 * shortage_ratio)`, doubled if the endpoint is under 30% of expected, and scaled by `1 + (v_kph - 25)/80` above 25 km/h. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/dec/dec.py` (`_calculate_slow_down`), `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/dec/constants.py`
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** Urgency goes through a `SmoothKalmanFilter` (measurement noise 0.1, process noise 0.1, smoothing factor 0.7); `has_slow_down` when the filtered urgency exceeds `SLOW_DOWN_PROB * 0.8 = 0.24`; urgency > 0.7 requests blended mode as an "emergency" (immediate switch, bypassing the 10-frame minimum mode duration and the 0.6 confidence hysteresis). — same `dec.py`
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** False-stop avoidance in DEC radar mode: a filtered lead (`LEAD_PROB = 0.45` over the lead filter) forces ACC unless standstill count > 3; standstill requests blended; MPC FCW forces blended. An invalid/short trajectory at > 20 km/h is itself treated as urgency 0.3. — same `dec.py` (`_radar_mode`, `_radarless_mode`)
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** The file header says "Version = 2025-6-30". — same `dec.py`

**sunnypilot E2E alerts (green light / lead departure)**
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** Green-light alert: armed only when stopped, no gas, not engaged (`not CC.enabled`), not moving in the last 2 s, and no lead; triggers when `modelV2.position.x[-1] > 30 m` (`GREEN_LIGHT_X_THRESHOLD`) continuously for > 0.3 s; one chime per stop (ARMED -> CONSUMED). — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/e2e_alerts_helper.py`

**FrogPilot Conditional Experimental Mode (CEM) and Force Stop**
- **[CODE-REMOTE]** Constants: `CITY_SPEED_LIMIT = 25` m/s; `CRUISING_SPEED = 5` m/s; `PLANNER_TIME = ModelConstants.T_IDXS[-1]`; `STOPPING_SPEED = 1.0` m/s ("planned speeds below this count as the model coming to a stop"); `STOP_TIME = PLANNER_TIME - 2`; `THRESHOLD = 1 - 1/e` (~0.632, commented "Requires the condition to be true for ~1 second"). — [frogpilot_variables.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/FrogPilot/frogpilot/common/frogpilot_variables.py)
- **[CODE-REMOTE]** `model_length = modelV2.position.x[-1]`; `model_stopped = model_length < CRUISING_SPEED * PLANNER_TIME` (i.e. the plan covers less distance than 5 m/s for the whole horizon); lead relevance requires `lead.dRel < (0 if standstill else model_length) + STOP_DISTANCE + LEAD_STOP_MARGIN`. — [frogpilot_planner.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/FrogPilot/frogpilot/controls/frogpilot_planner.py)
- **[CODE-REMOTE] (summarised fetch - re-verify)** CEM `stop_sign_and_light`: `SLOWDOWN_PERCENTAGE = 0.70` (0.75 once already detected, as hysteresis); `STOPPING_TIMES = [0.5, 1.5]` s; `model_slowing` = minimum planned velocity before `STOP_TIME` <= slowdown percentage x `v_ego`; `model_stopping` = planned velocity at the stopping times < `STOPPING_SPEED`; `stop_light_signal = model_slowing or model_stopping`; `stop_light_filter = FirstOrderFilter(0, 1.5, DT_MDL)` is updated with `stop_light_signal and not lead_relevant` and detection needs `filter.x >= THRESHOLD`; the filter resets when leaving standstill; all of it is disabled in "traffic mode". At standstill CEM uses `modelV2.action.shouldStop` (or `model_stopping` for classic longitudinal) to keep Experimental on. — [conditional_experimental_mode.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/FrogPilot/frogpilot/controls/lib/conditional_experimental_mode.py)
- **[CODE-REMOTE]** Force Stop (`update_force_stop`, quoted from source): `stop_detected = cem.stop_light_filter.x >= THRESHOLD and model_stopped`, and `not lead_relevant`; it must persist `>= 0.5 s` while not at standstill before `forcing_stop = True`; the stop distance is the first plan point with `velocity < 0.05` m/s (fallback: the position of the minimum planned velocity) and is then dead-reckoned down by `v_ego * DT_MDL` when the model stops predicting a stop. Gas or the accel button cancels and latches `override_force_stop`, which is only re-evaluated once the car is moving and the stop signal is gone. Disabled when not engaged, longitudinal paused, or traffic mode. — [frogpilot_vcruise.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/FrogPilot/frogpilot/controls/lib/frogpilot_vcruise.py)
- **[SECONDARY, 2024 - OLD]** A 2024 FrogPilot release note said the stop-light detection logic was revised so stop signs/lights trigger earlier, "up to about 200 m away at 80 km/h"; a March 2024 note said curve detection was tweaked to filter false positives at red lights. — [FrogPilot July-1st-2024 release](https://newreleases.io/project/github/FrogAi/FrogPilot/release/July-1st%2C-2024), [March-29th-2024 release](https://newreleases.io/project/github/FrogAi/FrogPilot/release/March-29th%2C-2024)

### Inferences
- The three "stop ahead" proxies in use are: (a) plan end-point distance short for the current speed (DEC; FrogPilot `model_stopped`), (b) planned velocity dropping to a fraction of ego speed within the horizon (CEM `model_slowing`), (c) planned velocity near zero in the next 0.5-1.5 s (CEM `model_stopping`). All are ratios or horizon-scaled, unlike this fork's fixed `MODEL_STOP_V = 1.5 m/s`, which is why they can fire early in an approach.
- FrogPilot's Force Stop exists precisely because the model alone often slows without fully stopping: it latches a stop distance once the model has predicted a stop for 0.5 s and holds it even if the model later changes its mind. That is the same failure the Fisker shadow drive saw from the other side.
- None of these distinguishes a stop sign from a red light, a queue, a speed bump, or a sharp turn; lead exclusion is the only class-level filter. A map prior is the natural complement.
- The comment "~1 second" on `THRESHOLD` does not match a 1.5 s time constant (reaching 1 - 1/e takes one time constant, i.e. ~1.5 s of continuously true signal).

## 3. How have forks combined maps with vision for stops; rolling stops, creep, stop line vs sign, queues, resume?

### Takeaway
I found no fork source that fuses a map stop node with the vision plan; FrogPilot and sunnypilot stop logic is vision-plan only, and the only map+vision design I could read in code is this fork's own Stop Sign Assist (map prior, model confirmation). The production reference designs (Tesla's 2020-era Traffic Light and Stop Sign Control; Mobileye REM) use the map for where to stop and which control applies, and require or assume driver confirmation to proceed.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** This fork's Stop Sign Assist: `COAST_START_M = 45.72` (150 ft), `A_COMFORT = 1.0` m/s^2, `COAST_TIME_S = 2.0`, `COAST_DV = 0.4` m/s, `LAG_S = 1.0`, `STOP_MARGIN_M = 6.0` (stop this far before the sign's map point), `STOPPED_V = 0.3`, `STOPPED_NEAR_M = 20.0`, `MODEL_STOP_V = 1.5`, GPS max age 2 s / accuracy 15 m. Until `confirmed`, the target is only `v_ego - 0.4` (coast); braking to `sqrt(2*A*d)` starts only after `model_wants_stop` (`action.shouldStop` or `min(velocity.x) < 1.5`). Gas override hands the sign to the driver. A direction filter exists in `stop_sign_direction.py`. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/stop_sign/stop_sign_controller.py`
- **[CODE-REMOTE]** FrogPilot handles queued vehicles by exclusion: both CEM's stop filter and Force Stop are gated by `not lead_relevant`, so the lead policy takes over behind a queue. Resume after a forced stop is driver-initiated (gas or accel button sets `override_force_stop`). — [frogpilot_vcruise.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/FrogPilot/frogpilot/controls/lib/frogpilot_vcruise.py)
- **[DOC]** Tesla Traffic Light and Stop Sign Control (2020-era Autopilot feature; OLD but still the clearest documented L2 design): the car stops at a red stop line shown on the visualisation unless the driver confirms by briefly pressing the accelerator or the stalk; confirmation was required even at green lights; after confirmation the line turns grey; a confirmation given before the car has stopped at a stop sign is ignored; the car "may not stop at a T-junction that has no stop sign or stop line, or if the junction is not included in the map data"; drivers "should never depend on this feature to decide whether to stop or proceed." — [Tesla Model Y owner's manual](https://www.tesla.cn/ownersmanual/modely/en_gb/GUID-A701F7DC-875C-4491-BC84-605A77EA152C.html), [Tesla Model X manual (manualslib)](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/2580181/Tesla-X-2015.html?page=116)
- **[DOC, vendor marketing]** Mobileye says its crowdsourced REM map supplies what vision and conventional HD maps do not: "which traffic light is relevant to the path on which it's driving" and "where is the best place to stop for an unobstructed view at an intersection"; its Cloud-Enhanced Driver-Assist "fuses real-time camera-based sensing information with ... REM-based AV maps." — [Mobileye: AV maps vs HD maps](https://www.mobileye.com/blog/av-maps-vs-hd-maps/), [Mobileye Cloud-Enhanced Driver-Assist](https://www.mobileye.com/blog/cloud-enhanced-driver-assist/)

### Inferences
- Two fusion orders exist. "Map prior, vision confirms" (this fork) suppresses false stops from stale/wrong-direction map nodes but inherits the model's reluctance to plan a full stop. "Map decides, driver confirms to proceed" (Tesla 2020) guarantees the stop and puts the false-positive cost on the driver as an accelerator tap. For a system whose model demonstrably does not confirm rolling stops, the second order (with direction filtering as the false-positive control) is the more robust default.
- A middle path consistent with the code found: require only a weak vision confirmation (e.g. plan end-point shortfall or planned-speed ratio as in DEC/CEM, not a near-zero planned speed) to arm braking, then latch the stop as Force Stop does.
- Stop position: OSM `highway=stop` nodes mark the sign (or sometimes the junction), not the stop line. This fork's fixed 6 m margin is a guess at that offset; FrogPilot instead uses the model's own predicted stop point (first plan point with v < 0.05 m/s), which could refine the map distance once the model does plan a stop.
- Creeping for visibility: nothing found in fork code. Tesla's documented design avoids it by handing the "go" decision to the driver.

- Older map-based forks (e.g. pre-2023 OSM-based stop/turn logic in community forks) were not researched; I have no sourced description of them.
- No source found for any fork implementing automatic creep-forward, all-way-stop turn-taking, or automatic resume from a stop sign.

## 4. Best practical confirmation signal for a map-prior stop in shadow mode; labelling ground truth and tuning from logs

### Takeaway
No published method was found; what the code shows is that the current confirmation (`shouldStop` or planned speed < 1.5 m/s) is structurally late or impossible for rolling stops, and that the forks' own proxies (plan end-point shortfall, planned-speed ratio, 0.5-1.5 s planned speed) are the candidates to evaluate. Everything below the first two bullets is inference and a proposed procedure, not a sourced result.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** `action.shouldStop` can only be true below 0.3 m/s ego speed. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/drive_helpers.py` lines 17-18
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** Shadow mode logs a `stop_sign` cloudlog event on each state change with `state, prev, sign, dist, v, v_target, confirmed, coast_at`; `confirmed` latches once and is never cleared within a sign. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/stop_sign/stop_sign_controller.py` lines 136-181
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** Candidate signals available every model frame: `position.x[-1]` (plan length), `velocity.x` (33 points), `acceleration.x`, `action.desiredAcceleration`, `meta.disengagePredictions.brakePressProbs` / `gasPressProbs` / `brakeDisengageProbs`, `meta.hardBrakePredicted`. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/cereal/log.capnp` lines 1113-1159
- **[CODE-LOCAL / CODE-REMOTE]** Thresholds other forks ship for the same proxies: DEC expected plan length 32 m at 0 km/h to 165 m at 60 km/h with filtered urgency > 0.24; FrogPilot planned min speed <= 0.70 x v_ego, planned speed < 1.0 m/s at 0.5-1.5 s, plan length < 50 m, 1.5 s filter >= 0.632, 0.5 s persistence. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/dec/constants.py`; [frogpilot_variables.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/FrogPilot/frogpilot/common/frogpilot_variables.py)
- **[DOC]** The only regulator-defined number for a "rolling stop" found: Tesla's recalled feature allowed travel through all-way stops at up to 5.6 mph. — [NHTSA Part 573 report 22V-037](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2022/RCLRPT-22V037-4462.PDF)

### Inferences
- **Ground-truth labels (proposed).** For each map sign pass, from `carState` within a window of roughly -80 m to +15 m of the stop point: minimum `vEgo`, distance at which the minimum occurs, peak deceleration, brake-pressed duration, and whether a lead was present (`radarState.leadOne`). Classes: full stop (min v < 0.3 m/s), rolling stop (0.3 to ~2.5 m/s, i.e. below the 5.6 mph figure), slow-through (clear deceleration but higher min speed), no response (no deceleration beyond coast: likely wrong-direction sign, bad map node, or a missed sign). Full + rolling are positives for "a stop was warranted"; no-response passes with driver attention plausible are the negatives that matter for false-stop tuning. Hand-review the negatives from video, since a driver running a real sign is also possible.
- **Per-pass model features (proposed).** As functions of distance-to-sign: `position.x[-1]` relative to DEC's expected distance; `min(velocity.x) / vEgo`; planned velocity at 0.5/1.5 s and at the time the plan reaches the map stop point; `action.desiredAcceleration`; `brakePressProbs`. Record the distance at which each first crosses a candidate threshold and stays crossed for N frames.
- **Tuning.** With labelled passes, sweep each threshold and report precision/recall plus the confirmation lead distance; the usable requirement is confirmation before the braking point `v^2/(2*1.0) + v*1.0` m ahead of the stop point, not confirmation at any time. Pick per-signal thresholds on recall of full+rolling stops at an acceptable false-confirm rate on negatives, then cross-check on held-out drives and after each model change.
- **Caveat on shadow data.** When the human is braking, the model sees the deceleration and its plan follows; a threshold tuned on driver-driven passes will look better than it performs when the system itself must initiate the stop. A fairer test set is passes where the model's signal crosses before the driver's first brake application.
- The single shadow drive is one data point; before changing design, confirm from the raw `modelV2.velocity.x` whether the 1.5 m/s test truly never fired or fired after the sign was released.

### Gaps
- No published methodology (comma, sunnypilot, FrogPilot) for tuning stop confirmation from logs was found.
- No data on how many labelled passes are needed; no public dataset of stop-sign approaches with openpilot model outputs was found.
- Whether `brakePressProbs` is still populated meaningfully by the 2026 models was not verified.

## 5. Intersection turns: turn desires, navigate-on-openpilot, angle-steering rate limits and override

### Takeaway
Turn desires (`turnLeft`/`turnRight`) are a model input that sunnypilot's Lane Turn Desire sets from the blinker below a configurable speed (hard-capped at 20 mph); comma has published no reliability figure for them. Navigate-on-openpilot was "temporarily" removed in 0.9.7 (June 2024) because the model's ability to execute the manoeuvres was limited, and I found no evidence it has returned. On this Fisker port, sharp turns are bounded by angle-rate limits that fall to 0.2 deg/frame at 25 m/s and by openpilot's generic 5 m/s^3 jerk / 3 m/s^2 lateral-accel / 0.2 1/m curvature clamps.

## 6. Human-factors and regulatory guidance for an L2 system that stops for signs automatically

### Takeaway
The firmest precedents are (a) NHTSA recall 22V-037, which treated a deliberate rolling stop of up to 5.6 mph as a safety defect even with no reported incidents, and (b) Tesla's documented design of stopping by default and requiring an explicit driver action to proceed. Defaults should therefore be: come to a complete stop, never auto-proceed from a stop sign, announce the stop early, and let a gas press override. Euro NCAP's 2026 assisted-driving protocols exist but I could not confirm any stop-sign/traffic-light-specific requirement.

### Cited Findings
- **[DOC]** NHTSA recall 22V-037 (Part 573 filed 27 Jan 2022, OLD): 53,822 Tesla vehicles; FSD Beta "rolling stop" functionality allowed the vehicle to travel through an all-way-stop intersection at up to 5.6 mph without first coming to a stop, under conditions including the feature being enabled in the FSD profile, an all-way stop, and speed below 5.6 mph; remedy was an OTA update disabling the function (firmware 2021.44.30.15); Tesla reported no known collisions, injuries, or fatalities. — [NHTSA RCLRPT-22V037-4462](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2022/RCLRPT-22V037-4462.PDF), [NHTSA recall acknowledgement RCAK-22V037](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2022/RCAK-22V037-9109.pdf)
- **[DOC]** Tesla Traffic Light and Stop Sign Control: stop is the default at every detected control; proceeding requires the driver to press the accelerator or stalk; a confirmation given before the car has stopped at a stop sign is ignored; the manual states the driver must not depend on the feature. — [Tesla Model Y owner's manual](https://www.tesla.cn/ownersmanual/modely/en_gb/GUID-A701F7DC-875C-4491-BC84-605A77EA152C.html)
- **[SECONDARY, 2020]** At launch, US behaviour let the car follow a lead through a green light without confirmation while Canada required confirmation regardless. — [CleanTechnica, June 2020](https://cleantechnica.com/2020/06/24/tesla-traffic-light-stop-sign-control-manual-updated/)
- **[DOC, headers only]** Euro NCAP's Assisted Driving (Highway & Interurban Assist) protocol v1.1 (October 2025, for January 2026 implementation) scores Assistance Competence (Driver Engagement balanced against Vehicle Assistance) and Safety Backup; a Safe Driving - Driver Engagement protocol v1.2 dated July 2026 also exists. I did not read their contents. — [Euro NCAP Assisted Driving v1.1](https://www.euroncap.com/media/91713/euro-ncap-protocol-assisted-driving-v11.pdf), [Euro NCAP Driver Engagement v1.2](https://cdn.euroncap.com/cars/assets/Euro_NCAP_Protocol_Safe_Driving_Driver_Engagement_v1_2_ebce03a443.pdf)
- **[DOC]** comma's own framing for Experimental-mode stopping: "you must stay attentive and be ready to take over at all times." — [openpilot 0.9.0](https://blog.comma.ai/090release/)
- **[CODE-LOCAL]** This fork already implements two of these patterns: gas press releases the sign to the driver, and sunnypilot's green-light / lead-departure features are chime-only prompts that fire only when stopped and not engaged. — `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/stop_sign/stop_sign_controller.py`; `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/e2e_alerts_helper.py`

### Inferences
- The recall is about what the system does, not what the driver did: a driver rolling through at 2.4 mph in shadow mode is normal human behaviour, but a system target of anything above standstill at a stop sign reproduces the recalled design. The stop target should be 0, with no "rolling stop" option.
- That also argues against using human behaviour as the confirmation standard: the map-prior stop should not be cancelled because the model (trained on humans who roll) does not plan a full stop.
- Driver-confirm-to-proceed (accelerator tap) is the conservative resume rule and matches the only documented production L2 design found; automatic resume from a stop sign has no precedent in the sources read.
- Announce early: a visible/audible "stopping for stop sign" state at coast start gives the driver the chance to override a wrong-direction or stale map node before braking begins.
