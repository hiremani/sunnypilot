# The ceiling of open-source openpilot-based driving (as of 26 November 2026)

Scope: what comma's openpilot and the community forks (sunnypilot, FrogPilot, dragonpilot, others) can do today, what is unreleased or experimental, how well it works, and what hardware it needs. The aim is to find what the Fisker Ocean port (a sunnypilot fork on a comma four) could adopt to close the gap to Tesla FSD.

Source note: some facts come from the local repository (`/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot`), which is sunnypilot 2026.002.x synced to openpilot v0.11.1. These are cited as local file paths.

## comma openpilot 0.10 / 0.11+ (2025-2026): longitudinal, lateral, lane change, navigation, models, roadmap, comma four and chestnut

### Takeaway
Since 0.10 (Aug 2025), openpilot's Experimental mode is fully end-to-end. A world-model-trained policy outputs the plan directly and the MPC planners are gone. Lights, stops, curves and traffic speed come from the model, not from map rules. The next release (0.11.3, unreleased in late Nov 2026) makes this "Autospeed" always on. comma's ceiling has moved to an external GPU ("chestnut", Aug 2026), which runs a model of about 1B parameters with "similar compute to Tesla HW4". comma has also said that future autonomy work will target chestnut-class models first. Navigation-following (turning at junctions on a route) is NOT in stock openpilot: it was removed in 0.9.7 (June 2024) and has not come back.

### Cited Findings
**Release timeline (driving-relevant)**
- 0.9.7 (2024-06-13): lateral model uses past curvature as input; follow-distance button sets the driving personality — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.9.7 (13 Jun 2024): "we temporarily remove Navigate on openpilot from Experimental mode". The reason given: the model could not reliably carry out the maneuvers needed to follow the route. comma said "We plan on bringing it back in a better state as soon as these improvements are achieved." Turn-by-turn navigation display stayed for prime/lite users — [comma blog 0.9.7](https://blog.comma.ai/097release/)
- Navigate on openpilot first appeared in 0.9.4 in Experimental mode. It fed the model a map image and nav instructions — [comma blog 0.9.7](https://blog.comma.ai/097release/); [0.9.4 post](https://blog.comma.ai/094release/)
- 0.9.0 introduced Experimental mode with end-to-end longitudinal control, including stopping at traffic lights and stop signs (alpha) — [comma blog 0.9.0](https://blog.comma.ai/090release/)
- 0.9.8 (2025-02-28): Chill mode gates positive acceleration; driver monitoring can run when not engaged; localizer no longer needs GPS at runtime — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.9.9 (2025-05-23): new training architecture that uses parts of MLSIM; steering actuation delay learned online; Tesla Model 3/Y added — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.10.0 (released 2025-08-05 in RELEASES.md; blog dated 21 Aug 2025): new "Tomb Raider" world-model training architecture, based on a CVPR paper. In Experimental mode, "end-to-end planning from the world model replaces longitudinal MPC", and lateral MPC is no longer the training target. Stopped-lead handling was improved for Chill mode. The share of ignored low-speed lead frames fell from 78% to 52% — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md); [comma blog 0.10](https://blog.comma.ai/010release/)
- 0.10 roadmap quote: future releases work toward openpilot 1.0, "where end-to-end longitudinal control graduates from Experimental to Chill mode" — [comma blog 0.10](https://blog.comma.ai/010release/)
- 0.10.1 (2025-09-08): world model no longer uses global localization inputs; 2x parameters; trained on 4x segments — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.10.2 (2025-11-19): comma four support. 0.10.3 (2025-12-17): new temporal policy architecture; noise model for on-policy training — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.11.0 (17 Mar 2026): "WMI" driving model trained on videos AND plans generated by a learned world model, described as "the first real-world robotics agent shipped to real users that was fully trained in a learned simulation". The simulator is a 2B-parameter diffusion transformer trained on 2.5M minutes of video. The policy is trained with lateral/longitudinal action and observation noise — [comma blog 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)
- 0.11.0 claimed results: users on nightly "now prefer Experimental mode (end-to-end longitudinal policy) over openpilot's ACC policy"; "much more confident at selecting an appropriate speed on the highway"; "noticeably improved reactivity around parked cars". These come only as charts, with no numbers. The post does not discuss traffic light or stop behaviour specifically — [comma blog 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)
- 0.11.0 lateral: Toyota RAV4 TSS2/SecOC moved to torque control. Angle-control cars got a steering arc that estimates torque from the lateral acceleration actually achieved. comma four standby power fell 77% to 52 mW — [comma blog 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)
- 0.11.1 (2026-05-18): new driver monitoring model and driver camera pipeline; comma four thermal policy; Rivian R1S/R1T 2025 — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- 0.11.2 (RELEASES.md date 2026-10-14): new 880M-parameter driving model; "large models can run on an external GPU". comma connect gains camera livestream and dashcam clips; VW ID.4 2021-25 and CUPRA Born added — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md). CONFLICT: the chestnut blog says the model for 0.11.2 is "1B parameter". Search aggregators gave 0.11.2 dates of 2026-06-15, 2026-08-12 and 2026-10-23 — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/); [aiwiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/openpilot)
- 0.11.3 (UNRELEASED as of this research): "Autospeed" is out of beta and always on. openpilot chooses speed from the scene: it stops at red lights, goes on green, slows for curves and matches traffic, and the cruise set speed becomes a maximum only. Also a new driver monitoring model with always-on monitoring and better sleep detection — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)

**chestnut (external GPU)**
- Announced 12 Aug 2026 (Gigazine covered it on 19 Aug). It is a compute upgrade for the comma four. Ready-to-drive kit: $799 with an AMD Radeon RX 9060 8GB plus power, cable and mount. "tiny chestnut": $249 for your own GPU. Both have a 30-day trial and a 1-year warranty — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260819-egpu-dock-chestnut/)
- Purpose: on-device models were held to a power budget of about 10 W; chestnut raises it to about 100 W. The first chestnut-class model has about 30x the parameters and 100x the FLOPs of the latest on-device model, and is "bigger than all openpilot driving models we've released combined". A comma four with chestnut has "similar compute to Tesla HW4" — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/); [hwbusters](https://hwbusters.com/news/comma-ai-egpu-dock-runs-open-source-firmware-249-bare-799-with-an-rx-9060/)
- Roadmap quote: "Going forward, our autonomy team will focus primarily on maximizing the capabilities of chestnut-class models." — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/)
- Install: the GPU goes in the passenger footwell or under the passenger seat, powered from the 12 V / cigarette-lighter socket, with a cable to the comma four. The dock is PCIe Gen4 x4 to USB4 with open-source firmware — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/); [Phoronix](https://phoronix.com/news/Comma.ai-PCIe-Gen4-USB4-Dock)
- Fleet scale: nearly 1 million driving minutes per day, about 15% of drives used for training — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/)

**Lane change (stock openpilot / sunnypilot code)**
- A lane change needs the turn signal and, in stock openpilot, a steering nudge. Minimum speed is 20 mph (`LANE_CHANGE_SPEED_MIN = 20 * CV.MPH_TO_MS`) and the maneuver times out after 10 s. The car's blind-spot signal (`carstate.leftBlindspot/rightBlindspot`) blocks the change — local `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/desire_helper.py` lines 11-12, 68-77
- The openpilot docs say openpilot cannot look beside the car or check the blind spot during lane changes (older LIMITATIONS.md) — [LIMITATIONS.md (old devel copy)](https://github.com/cfranyota/openpilot-retired/blob/devel-0.9.1-1/docs/LIMITATIONS.md)

### Inferences
- comma's strategy is "everything from the model". Lights, stops, curves and lead speed come from the end-to-end policy, not from map or rule layers. This differs from sunnypilot/FrogPilot, which add map (OSM) rules on top. For the Ocean, the cheapest gain is to track upstream models (0.11.2's large model needs chestnut; 0.11.3 Autospeed).
- Junction turns on a route are the biggest open gap in stock openpilot. No current comma release follows a route; the nav input was removed in 2024.
- chestnut puts HW4-class compute on any comma four car for $249-$799. comma says future autonomy work will go first to chestnut-class models, so an on-device-only setup will likely fall behind.

### Gaps
- The exact 0.11.2 release date and model size (880M in RELEASES.md vs 1B in the blog) are unresolved. The source of the 0.11.2 RELEASES.md date also conflicts with aggregator dates.
- No comma metric (for example, disengagements per mile or red-light stop success rate) was found for Experimental mode or Autospeed.
- I found no published date for 0.11.3, and no statement that it brings Navigate on openpilot back.
- comma four compute/sensor specs were not covered in the release posts I fetched.

## sunnypilot and notable forks (FrogPilot, dragonpilot, others): navigation, map turns, speed limits, stop-and-go, lights, lane changes

### Takeaway
sunnypilot (latest 2026.002.003, 20 Nov 2026, synced to openpilot v0.11.1) keeps openpilot's model and adds rule layers. These are MADS (lateral without long), Speed Limit Assist (from OSM, car, nav or vision, in a set priority), Smart Cruise Control (vision and map curve speed), ICBM (cruise button emulation for openpilot long), NNLC, auto lane change timers with blind-spot abort, and model selection. FrogPilot adds Conditional Experimental Mode, which switches into the end-to-end mode for curves, stopped leads, predicted stops and nav-route intersections, plus a Mapbox/OSM speed-limit controller and nudgeless lane changes. No fork found steers through a turn at a junction on a navigation route; nav data is used only to change speed or mode.

### Cited Findings
- sunnypilot release history: 2026.001.000 (2026-05-06) is a full UI rewrite from Qt to Raylib Python with comma four support and an OSM panel. 2026.002.000 (2026-06-28) is synced to openpilot v0.11.1. 2026.002.002 (Aug 7) changes mapd/plannerd health. 2026.002.003 (Nov 20, 2026) is a fresh-install fix. None of these releases adds a new navigation, lane-change or traffic-light feature — [sunnypilot releases](https://github.com/sunnypilot/sunnypilot/releases); local `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/CHANGELOG.md`
- So as of Nov 2026 sunnypilot ships openpilot v0.11.1 models. The 0.11.2 large/external-GPU model and 0.11.3 Autospeed have not yet reached a sunnypilot release (inferred from the sync line above) — [sunnypilot releases](https://github.com/sunnypilot/sunnypilot/releases)
- sunnypilot Speed Limit Assist: sets cruise speed from detected limits. Sources are OpenStreetMap, the car's own system (some cars) and others, and the user sets the priority (e.g. OSM, Navigation, Vision). It has offsets and automatic or confirm modes. The OSM implementation was refactored to use fewer resources, with weekly map updates — [batpilot SLA docs (sunnypilot-derived)](https://batpilot.github.io/batpilot/features/speed-limit-assist/)
- sunnypilot "Enhanced Speed Control": cruise speed is adjusted by the vision model, OSM data and/or Speed Limit Control without user input — [sunnypilot (asiusai mirror README)](https://github.com/asiusai/sunnypilot)
- sunnypilot UI/features in 2026.001.000: Speed Limit Assist status, Smart Cruise Control elements, dynamic ICBM status, blind-spot indicators, MADS lateral-only states, NNLC — local `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/CHANGELOG.md` lines 33-138
- sunnypilot auto lane change: `AutoLaneChangeMode` setting (OFF / nudge / timed nudgeless). It aborts when the blind spot is occupied or a road edge is detected (`left_edge_detected`). A "lane turn controller" uses blind-spot inputs too — local `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/desire_helper.py` lines 44-77
- FrogPilot Conditional Experimental Mode: switches to Experimental mode for curves, slower or stopped leads, driving below a set speed, predicted stops, and approaching intersections/turns while navigating. It returns to Chill when none apply. A release note says it now triggers for detected stop lights/signs even in curves — [FrogPilot GitHub](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot); [DeepWiki FrogPilot features](https://deepwiki.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/1.1-frogpilot-features-and-enhancements)
- FrogPilot Speed Limit Controller: uses offline map data, online Mapbox data and the car dashboard. If no limit is known, the fallback is keep set speed, last limit, or Experimental mode — [FrogPilot GitHub](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot)
- FrogPilot lane changes: nudgeless lane changes with lane detection to avoid curbs; "Automatic Lane Changes" on selected vehicles; blind-spot path drawn on the UI; lateral can pause below a set speed, when braking or with the turn signal on — [FrogPilot GitHub](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot); [DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/1.1-frogpilot-features-and-enhancements)
- FrogPilot has no 2026 release in the search results (the latest found is Aug 2025). The development status in 2026 is unknown — [FrogPilot releases](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot/releases)
- An older fork (arnepilot, based on dragonpilot) had automatic lane change gated by BSM. It waits about 1 s, and if BSM detects an object it aborts and returns to the original lane — [arne182/openpilot](https://github.com/arne182/openpilot)
- AnimalPilot fork: auto lane change timer after the blinker — [AnimalPilot](https://github.com/AlexandreSato/animalpilot)
- dragonpilot: no current feature list was found — [dragonpilot docs README (old)](https://github.com/dingliangxue/dragonpilot/blob/docs/README.md)

### Inferences
- The fork ecosystem's ceiling is "openpilot model plus map and rule overlays for speed". This is roughly what the Ocean port already has or is building (stop signs from OSM, turn slowing, speed profile). No fork has shown map-guided steering through junctions.
- Blind-spot-aware lane changes in all forks depend on the car's own BSM signals (corner radars read via CAN), not on openpilot perception.

## Evidence of performance and known limits

### Takeaway
Independent ratings are old. Consumer Reports tested the comma two in Nov 2020. Press reported it near the top, but CR's own ranking put Super Cruise first, citing driver monitoring. No later CR ranking includes openpilot. comma publishes usage/preference charts but no disengagement statistics. Hard limits are fixed in code and safety: L2 only, driver monitoring always required, lateral accel about 3 m/s², longitudinal +2.0/-3.5 m/s².

### Cited Findings
- Consumer Reports (Nov 2020) tested 18 systems, 17 factory and one aftermarket comma two. The Drive reported openpilot "came out on top" — [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/37833/consumer-reports-ranks-this-aftermarket-driver-assistance-kit-above-tesla-autopilot-cadillac-super-cruise); CR's own article ranks Super Cruise first, mainly for driver monitoring — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/car-safety/cadillac-super-cruise-outperforms-other-active-driving-assistance-systems-a1113486809)
- Later CR rankings (2023: BlueCruise first, 12 systems) do not include openpilot — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2023/01/25/ford-gm-consumer-reports-driver-assistance-rankings-tesla/); [KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/consumer-reports-ford-makes-the-best-hands-free-system-tesla-slips-to-7th)
- openpilot is an ACC + ALC "failsafe passive system" that requires an alert driver. It is made to Level 2 standards; it is not autonomous and not collision avoidance; "openpilot may disengage without warning". Brake or cancel must give back control at once. The lateral limit follows ISO 11270/15622 ("0.9 seconds of maximum actuation to achieve a 1m lateral deviation") — [openpilot SAFETY.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/docs/SAFETY.md)
- Numeric limits in code: `ISO_LATERAL_ACCEL = 3.0` m/s², `MAX_LATERAL_JERK` about 3.6 m/s³ (opendbc lateral). Longitudinal is `ACCEL_MAX = 2.0`, `ACCEL_MIN = -3.5` m/s² — local `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/lateral.py` line 10, 18; `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/interfaces.py` lines 28-29
- Driver monitoring: a new model in 0.11.1; 0.11.3 (unreleased) makes monitoring always-on with better sleep detection — [RELEASES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/RELEASES.md)
- comma's own evidence for 0.11: a chart showing users prefer Experimental mode, with no numbers — [comma blog 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)

### Inferences
- The 3 m/s² lateral limit means openpilot cannot take tight urban turns (e.g. a 90° turn at a junction) by itself on most cars. This is a structural gap to FSD that no model upgrade fixes without a change to the safety limits.

### Gaps
- No independent 2025-2026 tests (CR, IIHS, press) of openpilot 0.10/0.11 were found.
- No published disengagement-rate or intervention metrics from comma.

## Extra sensors (corner radars, lidar, surround cameras) with openpilot

### Takeaway
I found no published openpilot or fork integration of lidar, extra corner radars or surround cameras into perception or planning. openpilot uses its own road cameras plus the car's forward radar where present. Forks use the car's existing BSM (corner radar) only as a boolean through CAN, to block lane changes and show UI. comma's 2026 direction is more compute (chestnut), not more sensors.

### Cited Findings
- openpilot keeps stock features including FCW, AEB, blind-spot warning and side collision warning. It does not replace them — [openpilot README via fork](https://github.com/arne182/openpilot)
- Forks use car BSM to abort lane changes (arnepilot) and to draw a blind-spot path (FrogPilot) — [arne182/openpilot](https://github.com/arne182/openpilot); [FrogPilot](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot)
- sunnypilot/openpilot code reads only `leftBlindspot`/`rightBlindspot` booleans from carState for lane changes — local `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/openpilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/desire_helper.py` line 68
- comma's stated path to bigger capability is a desktop GPU (about 100 W) running larger vision models, with "similar compute to Tesla HW4" (Tesla is also camera-only) — [comma chestnut blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/)

### Inferences
- For the Ocean, its own ADAS sensors (corner radars, surround cameras) could feed only through CAN signals the car already sends, such as BSM booleans and radar tracks. A deeper fusion would be original work with no community precedent.

### Gaps
- The search found no fork, paper or comma post on lidar or surround-camera fusion with openpilot. Community Discord work may exist but is not citable.
