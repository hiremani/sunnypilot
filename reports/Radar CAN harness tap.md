# Silence the panda before tapping the radar

The best place to tap the Ocean's mid-range radar (MRR) link is the ADAS controller connector **`C003B*6F`, pin 9 (CAN-H, white/brown) and pin 1 (CAN-L, grey/brown)**, in the cargo area. There, land the pair on the comma harness's spare **CAN1 pair (pink = H, blue = L)**. That puts the radar on panda bus 1, where the port already expects it. The other two places are worse. The radar connector at the bumper means taking the bumper off, works on a sealed connector exposed to water, and risks a radar recalibration. The two inline joints (`I141B*1`, `I136A*1`) are in places no document gives. The wiring source is a community copy of the MY2023 FM29 wiring set, kept locally at `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/service_docs/` ([Wiring set](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SY0NpmfMqZCx2eLu4gMEKs2z0kN_mZQT/view)). It gives the connector housing as **Molex Stac64 34729-0162**. This corrects the earlier camera-tap report, which gave `560123-0200`; that number belongs to the driver-camera LED connector. **No pin, colour or part in this report has been checked on a car.** The biggest hazard is in software, not in the wiring. In the Fisker safety mode the panda's bus-1 controller is a normal CAN node, not a listener: it sends ACK bits and, if its bit timing or the signal is wrong, error frames that can destroy radar frames. A small firmware change that keeps bus 1 in the controller's bus-monitoring mode must come before any connection. After that, the sequence is: prove the pins with a true listen-only CAN-FD tool, confirm the bit rates, take a fault-code baseline, connect one change at a time, and use radar leads only after the logs and the car's own fault flags stay clean. The user's Vgate vLinker FD+ is a classic-CAN adapter for Ford cars. It belongs in the OBD port for code reads and must never touch the radar pair.

## The link runs about 7.8 m, and the cargo-area end is the right one

### What the drawings show

The forward radar is the "Mid Range Radar – Front – Center". It clips to the front bumper, and the service manual removes the whole bumper before it unplugs the radar. After a radar is fitted, the manual calls for programming and calibration with the Fisker Aftersales Service Tool ([Service manual p.80-4/80-5, p.60-2 to 60-5](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1BwRtG41yIPEU5H6KvpWaj1X8OWSvLYuf/view)). The radar's own connector is **`S015A*1F`, a Molex Mini50 sealed 10-way receptacle, 34967-1001**. Only four pins are drawn: **3 = Private CAN-H, 2 = Private CAN-L, 9 = ignition-switched power (fuse RF33, 10 A), 6 = ground** ([Wiring set p.28](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SY0NpmfMqZCx2eLu4gMEKs2z0kN_mZQT/view); [RS 34967-1001](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/p/automotive-connectors/1367814)). The power is ignition-switched, so the link is live only with the car on.

The "ADAS BUS" sheet traces the pair through three harness sections. The body harness (W2107) runs from the controller to inline joint **`I141B*1` pins 77/85**. The engine-room harness (W2105) runs to inline joint **`I136A*1` pins 10/11**. The bumper harness (W2123) runs to the radar's pins 3/2. All three sections use **white/brown (H) and grey/brown (L), 0.35 mm²**, and the drawing shows twisted-pair symbols at both ends ([Wiring set p.10](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SY0NpmfMqZCx2eLu4gMEKs2z0kN_mZQT/view)). The segment numbers on the sheet (4055, 2088 and 1633) are probably lengths in millimetres. That would give a point-to-point link of roughly **7.8 m**, but the sheet does not state the unit.

At the controller ("Hydra3", FM2980140120B, behind the lower side trim of the rear compartment, fuse MF07 15 A), the pair ends on **`C003B*6F` cavity 9 (`A*9B`, MRR CAN-H) and cavity 1 (`A*1B`, MRR CAN-L)**. The same 16-way connector carries the four corner-radar pairs (FL 10/11, RL 12/13, FR 2/3, RR 4/5) and the driver-camera LED pair (16/15). Its housing is **Molex 34729-0162**, a "Stac64 receptacle housing, 16 circuits, dual row, polarization C, brown" ([Wiring set p.28](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SY0NpmfMqZCx2eLu4gMEKs2z0kN_mZQT/view); [Octopart 34729-0162](https://octopart.com/part/molex/34729-0162); [Service manual p.80-1](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1BwRtG41yIPEU5H6KvpWaj1X8OWSvLYuf/view)). The local copy of this sheet, `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/service_docs/wiring.pdf`, also corrects the earlier report: `560123-0200` is printed under the driver-camera LED connector `V009B*1F`, not under the controller.

### Why the controller end wins

| Tap point | Access | Environment | What a mistake costs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`C003B*6F` pins 9/1, cargo area** | Remove the rear-compartment trim. No bumper work. | Dry, inside the cabin | Opening the connector drops all five radar links and the driver-camera LED. A splice touches only the MRR pair. | **Use this** |
| Radar `S015A*1F` pins 3/2, bumper | Remove the bumper with a helper | Wet, salt, impact zone | Radar recalibration if the radar moves; a leak path into a sealed connector | Last resort |
| `I136A*1` pins 10/11 or `I141B*1` pins 77/85 | Location not documented | Unknown | Large inline joints, unknown ownership | Not recommended |

Three facts make the cargo-area end the right choice. Its colour pair, white/brown + grey/brown, is the only one of the five radar pairs with those colours, so the right pair is easy to find by sight. A splice there is in a dry place. And the comma's camera-splice harness already works on the ADAS controller's CAN, so the new pair has a short run to the harness. The other ends of the private link carry the same frames, because one bus carries both directions: the controller sends time sync, ego motion and wheel speeds to the radar (0x100, 0x200, 0x201, 0x202), and the radar sends its header and 32 object slots every 65 ms (0x300, 0x310–0x32F) ([local DBC](/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/opendbc_repo/opendbc/dbc/fisker_ocean_mrr.dbc)).

No public write-up shows how the community's first capture was tapped. The radar DBC credits one 61-second parking-lot route as its only source, and the port code says only that bus 1 is the radar link "when the harness taps it" ([local `values.py`](/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/fisker/values.py)). In this car the bus is not reached today. Across **89 drives and 510 segments, bus 1 received zero frames**, and pandaStates shows bus 1 with rx 0, tx 0 and no errors at 500/2000 kbit/s ([local Phase 0 notes](/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/research_notes/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md)). The controller's own MRR fault bits (0x481, 0x482, 0x487) read zero on the three drives decoded. So the radar link probably works; the comma is just not wired to it.

## A Y-adapter or a staggered splice, with no third terminator

### Getting the pair to panda bus 1

comma's harness build guide says that the 18-pin harness connector carries three CAN pairs. **CAN1 ("CAN1_H-PINK", "CAN1_L-BLUE") passes straight through the harness box to the device.** The box "cannot intercept" CAN1, and the guide says CAN1 is "typically… connected to the radar" ([commaai BUILD_HARNESS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/hardware/master/harness/BUILD_HARNESS.md)). The harness box needs no change. The car-side harness needs only the pink and blue wires added to its 18-pin connector. comma sells a "Development" harness-connector option that has the 18-pin Molex connector and all its wires, and names Molex 0638192300 as the crimp tool ([comma shop](https://comma.ai/shop/harness-connector); [BUILD_HARNESS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/hardware/master/harness/BUILD_HARNESS.md)). Rivian owners did the same thing: a harness change that adds the CAN1 pair, plus a pigtail at a second connector ([rivianforums](https://rivianforums.com/forum/threads/comma-ai-openpilot-autopilot-progress-on-r1.37798/post-751877)).

In the panda firmware, harness flip swaps only buses 0 and 2. Bus 1's mapping is fixed, but on the comma four board file the choice of transceiver is XORed with the detected flip (`panda/board/boards/tres.h:57-93`, `cuatro.h:128`). So the first live capture must show radar IDs on **src 1** before anything else is trusted.

### Two ways to make the junction

The preferred method in professional practice is a connector-level Y harness made from the OEM connector family's own housings and terminals. Telematics installers say the same: use a vehicle-specific T-harness when one exists ([Geotab](https://support.geotab.com/harnesses/installation/doc/harness-cheat); [NHTSA MC-10207266](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10207266-0001.pdf)). At `C003B*6F` that means a short adapter. One end is a **34729-0162 receptacle with CTX64 34803-series terminals** that plugs into the controller. Molex 0348033212 is a CTX64 tin terminal listed "Use With 34729" ([Molex drawing via Mouser](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/276/0348033212_CRIMP_TERMINALS-701701.pdf)). The other end is a male polarization-C mate that the car's own receptacle plugs into. All 16 circuits pass straight through, and only pins 9/1 branch off to the comma. One catch: Stac64 is a wire-to-board family whose receptacles mate with 34690/34691 PCB headers ([RS Stac64](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/p/automotive-connectors/7241438)). So the male side is probably a small PCB with a polarization-C header, not a wire-to-wire plug. Neither the header's part number nor the existence of a wire-to-wire mate has been confirmed. The Y keeps the OEM harness intact and fully reversible. The cost is that **all five radar links and the driver-camera LED then pass through the adapter**, and that every trial opens a connector that serves the whole radar suite.

A splice touches only the MRR pair, but it leaves permanent joints in the OEM harness. Tesla's service rules are a good stand-in for OEM practice. Do not make an inline repair within 100 mm of a connector. Stagger the splices. Keep CAN-H and CAN-L the same length. Never splice inside grommets or areas that move. Tape 50 mm each side ([Tesla Model 3 harness repair](https://service.tesla.com/docs/Model3/ServiceManual/en-us/GUID-3B58243E-5655-4F56-9D8F-0CDAACAF9BA8.html)). Untwist no more than about 30–40 mm and keep the rest twisted ([HP Academy](https://www.hpacademy.com/courses/canbus-communications-decoded/practical-discussion-splicing-together-new-canbus-wires)). Solder sleeves or adhesive-lined crimp splices suit the job ([RS solder sleeve guide](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/content/discovery/ideas-and-advice/guide-to-solder-sleeves)). Do not use IDC "Scotchlok" taps. On 0.35 mm² wire they can cut strands, let water in and loosen under vibration ([Garage Journal](https://www.garagejournal.com/forum/threads/why-do-scotchlocks-have-such-a-bad-reputation.539967/latest)). Contactless clamp readers are not an option either, because they do not support CAN-FD ([CSS Electronics](https://csselectronics.com/products/contactless-can-bus-reader-adapter)).

### Termination and the stub problem

A point-to-point link is terminated at each end, so H to L should read **about 60 Ω** with the car off ([NI CAN termination guide](https://www.ni.com/en/support/documentation/supplemental/09/can-physical-layer-and-termination-guide.html)). **Add no terminator at the tap.** A third 120 Ω would drop the link to about 40 Ω, shrink the signal and add a discontinuity. Neither the comma's CAN1 path nor any test tool may terminate this pair.

The stub is the real electrical question. ISO 11898-2's often-quoted 0.3 m stub limit is a 1 Mbit/s classic-CAN figure. TI's rule of thumb (twice the stub's delay no more than a third of the transceiver edge time) gives about 1.3 m for a 40 ns edge ([TI SLLA486](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SLLA486A/GUID-66620DAB-AFC0-41A9-B934-92931CA2ED3D)). NXP shows that ringing from unterminated stubs is what "effectively limited" CAN-FD to 2 Mbit/s outside point-to-point links. The pass test is that the recessive differential settles below 0.5 V before the sample point, at 70–80 % of the bit ([NXP CANSIGIMP](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/white-paper/CANSIGIMP-WP.pdf)). The uncomfortable part for this design is that **the panda's transceiver is in the comma device, not at the tap**. The stub therefore runs from the splice through the car harness, the harness box and the device cable to the windshield, which is probably several metres. That is far past the 0.3–1.3 m guidance. The earlier community capture shows that the panda can receive the link this way. It does not show that the radar and controller see a clean signal with that stub on the line. This is the main reason the plan below puts a scope on the link and reads the controller's fault flags before trusting the tap. If the scope shows ringing at the data-phase sample point, the fallback is a listen-only transceiver within about 10 cm of the splice, carrying the data to the comma some other way, rather than a longer panda stub. From the tap to the harness, use unshielded twisted pair, 0.35–0.5 mm², near 120 Ω, twisted like the OEM pair. Route it away from 12 V PWM and HV lines.

## The panda ACKs on bus 1 today, so firmware comes first

The port's software already refuses to transmit on bus 1. Generic forwarding gives bus 1 the destination −1. `fisker_fwd_hook` handles only buses 0 and 2. The two bus-1 entries in the TX allow-list (0x5D0 and 0x521, the candidate pools) are always rejected with `tx = false; // panda-internal: never transmit onto the radar link` ([local `fisker.h`](/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/opendbc_repo/opendbc/safety/modes/fisker.h); [local `safety.h`](/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/opendbc_repo/opendbc/safety/safety.h)). The logs agree: the 112,416 bus-1 frames in one route are all rejected echoes on src 193, and bus-1 totalTxCnt is 0.

Refusing to transmit frames is not the same as being silent. In `panda/board/main.c`, every car safety mode, Fisker included, sets `can_silent = false`. Only `SAFETY_SILENT` sets it true. The flag is global and applies to every bus through `can_set_speed()` in `drivers/fdcan.h`. `llcan_set_speed()` sets the FDCAN `MON` bit ("bus monitoring") only when `silent` is true (`stm32h7/llfdcan.h:105-108`). So while openpilot is engaged, the panda's bus-1 controller is a full participant on the radar link. It drives the ACK slot on every good frame. If it sees an error, it sends an active error flag that destroys the frame for both real nodes. Kvaser describes the difference: a silent node "does not transmit… ACK… error frames, or any other frames" ([Kvaser: silent mode](https://kvaser.com/knowledge-base/what-is-silent-mode/)). An extra ACK on a two-node link is harmless. The error flags are the danger. They would come from a bit-rate or sample-point mismatch, or from stub ringing at the panda's own input. Error flags raise the radar's error counters and can push it toward error-passive or bus-off. On this car they would probably show up as the controller's MRR E2E or lost-communication codes.

The fix is small. Add a bus-1 listen-only flag and OR it into the silent argument only for bus 1:

```c
// drivers/can_common.h
bool can_bus1_listen_only = false;

// main.c, set_safety_mode(): in the default (car-mode) branch
can_bus1_listen_only = (mode_copy == SAFETY_FISKER);
// and false in the SILENT / NOOUTPUT / ELM327 branches

// drivers/fdcan.h, can_set_speed()
can_silent || ((bus_number == 1U) && can_bus1_listen_only)
```

This keeps bus 1 in monitoring mode in the Fisker mode and leaves other brands alone. Ford, Rivian and Tesla use bus 1 for buses they do transmit on (`opendbc/car/ford/fordcan.py`, `rivian/radar_interface.py`, `tesla/values.py`). In MON mode the controller sends only recessive bits, so the existing `tx = false` rejection becomes a second guard rather than the only one. Two more guards belong with the change. First, a safety test that fails if any frame with bus == 1 is ever allowed. No radar or bus-1 test appears in `opendbc/safety/tests/test_fisker.py`. Second, a check that bus-1 `totalTxCnt` stays 0 on every drive. Prove the change on the bench before the car. Put the comma, in Fisker mode, on a two-node bench bus whose only other node is a single transmitter. If the panda ACKs, the transmitter sees ACKs. If MON works, the transmitter reports ACK errors, because nothing else acknowledges. Build and install the firmware through the normal deploy workflow. pandad's flashing of a locally built, unsigned firmware on the comma four has not been checked here, so confirm it before you depend on it.

## Bit rates are inferred at 500k/2M and must be measured

No Fisker or Magna document states the link's bit rates. The link is certainly CAN-FD: the sheet labels it "CANFD_MRR", and the DBC frames are 12–48 bytes long. The panda's defaults for every bus are **500 kbit/s arbitration and 2 Mbit/s data** (`panda/board/drivers/can_common.h:129-131`). No Fisker code calls `set_can_speed_kbps` or `set_data_speed_kbps`, so the community capture must have decoded at those defaults. That is strong evidence for 500k/2M, but it is not a measurement. The panda's allowed data rates include 1, 2 and 5 Mbit/s (`stm32h7/llfdcan.h:4-5`). If the link turns out to use a rate outside that list, the panda cannot listen to it at all.

Confirm the rates three ways before the panda joins the link. First, the listen-only tool at 500k/2M, ISO CAN-FD, should decode 0x300 and 0x310–0x32F at about 15.4 Hz with zero error frames. If it decodes nothing, try other data rates in monitor mode only; a wrong rate in monitor mode costs nothing. Second, a scope should show bit times of about 2 µs in arbitration and 0.5 µs in the data phase. Third, after the panda is connected, `pandaStates[].canState1` should show canSpeed/canDataSpeed 500/2000, `canfdEnabled` and `brsEnabled` true (they latch on the first FD/BRS frame), and totalErrorCnt and busOffCnt at 0. The rates are not the only timing parameter. Kvaser notes that error frames usually mean a mismatch in bit rate, sample point or SJW ([Kvaser bus configuration](https://kvaser.com/lesson/bus-configuration/)). A clean listen-only decode proves that the rate and sample point are good enough to receive. It does not prove that the panda's timing matches the OEM nodes'. That is one more reason to keep the panda in MON mode permanently.

## Prove pins with a listener, then add one change at a time

### Tools that may and may not touch the pair

Pin-proving needs a device that **never drives the bus**: not for ACK, not for error flags, not even at power-up. The candidates are a Kvaser CAN-FD interface in silent mode ([Kvaser](https://kvaser.com/knowledge-base/what-is-silent-mode/)); a PEAK PCAN-USB FD in listen-only mode, with one caution, because Linux-CAN developers report that some PEAK devices transmit while in "monitor" mode ([linux-can thread](https://lore.kernel.org/all/20210504100746.GB27660@x1.vandijck-laurijssen.be/)), so test its silence on the bench first; a CANable 2.0 running candleLight-FD firmware in listen-only mode (no source was checked for its silent behaviour, so bench-test it the same way); or the comma itself held in `SAFETY_SILENT`, which sets MON on every bus. Every one of them must have its termination off.

**The Vgate vLinker FD+ must not touch the radar pair.** It is an ELM327/STN-class adapter made for Ford's FORScan. The "FD" refers to Ford's HS-CAN and MS-CAN, not to CAN-FD, and its listings show classic CAN with no CAN-FD support ([manuals.plus vLinker FD](https://manuals.plus/ae/1005005771222403); [manuals.plus B08FSNVZVK](https://manuals.plus/asin/B08FSNVZVK); [product listing](https://m.reach.dog/shop/thecustomgiftspot/products/vlinker-fd-obd2-bluetooth-scan-tool-diagnostic-code-reader-for-ios-android-and-windows-made-for-forscan)). A classic CAN controller sees CAN-FD frames as format errors and can send error frames, which is the exact failure this plan exists to prevent. Its job is the OBD port. The Fiskeretta tool uses it to read the ADAS controller with UDS at 0x7A6/0x7AE (service 0x19, status byte kept) and to clear with 0x14 ([Fiskeretta](https://github.com/Fiskeretta/fiskeretta-diagnostics)). It may also read ECU identification. Whether the Ocean's diagnostic port answers on classic CAN for these requests has not been confirmed first-hand, so the baseline stage must prove it. Unplug the adapter when you are not using it, because a Bluetooth OBD dongle left in the port is an attack surface.

### Faults the car will raise, and how to watch them

The controller monitors its MRR link with its own flags, sent every 100 ms in 0x481 and 0x482, and the FCM has its own flags in 0x487. The DBC comments give the matching DTC names: **U322687** (ADAS–MRR lost), **U134B87** (private-CAN lost), **U134B82** (private-CAN E2E), **C12A609** (bus hardware error), **C12AE86** (invalid signal), **U30EA87** (FCM–MRR lost) and **C10F681** (FCM DLC fault). `ADAS_SysFltTyp` = 3 in 0x317 means "Radar" (`/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/oceanscape/dbc/fisker_ocean_adas_world.dbc`). No local file gives these codes' persistence or healing rules. The controller's comparable monitors use a 200 ms missing-message timeout, 3 E2E errors in a row, a 30 ms bus-off window, and healing with 40-cycle ageing. The camera codes set after 500 ms and have no documented healing. So plan as if an MRR code holds until at least the next ignition cycle and costs ACC, AEB and FCW. The manual names radar as an AEB input, but the exact feature loss is an inference. This port has a precedent: frames that reached the ADAS module as a second sender latched `ADAS_Sts_ACC_ICC` into a fault until an ignition cycle (`fisker.h:907-918`). The comma itself is the best live indicator. It already logs bus 2, where 0x481, 0x482, 0x317 and the FCM's 0x487 all appear. Note one existing condition for the baseline: bus 2 already shows **26.3 M errors and 54 bus-offs** on one route from the camera-side splice. Judge bus-1 errors separately from that.

### Software consequences of turning radar on

Once the fingerprint sees 0x300 on bus 1, `radarUnavailable` turns false, radar tracks reach radard, and model leads that match a track become `radar: True`. Fisker disables radard's radar-only low-speed lead, because the MRR has no elevation and reads gantries as stopped cars (`radard.py:260-265`). The fingerprint window is only about 1–2 s (`car_helpers.py`). A radar that wakes late leaves that drive vision-only. The larger risk is a different one: **an intermittent tap is worse than no tap.** With openpilot longitudinal on, any mid-drive loss of radar frames makes `can_valid` false. That raises `canError`, which is IMMEDIATE_DISABLE (`selfdrived.py:459-465`, `events.py:753-760`). Before the tap that fault could not happen, because the base interface returned empty radar data.

## Build and verification checklist

Each stage changes one thing and holds longer than the slowest monitor. The stop conditions apply at every stage.

| # | Stage | Action | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|

**Stop and roll back** on any of these: an MRR bit in 0x481/0x482/0x487; SysFltTyp = 3; any new ADAS or MRR code (pending, confirmed or failing); bus-1 errors, bus-off or a non-zero txCnt; missing or irregular radar frames; a telltale or a change in ACC or AEB availability; or `radarUnavailable` changing between drives. **Rollback:** with the car asleep, remove the Y, or cut the stub back and insulate it at the splice, and restore the factory connection. Switch on and confirm the MRR bits are 0 and bus 1 is silent again. The software falls back to vision-only by itself, which is the state proven over 89 drives. Read codes and check bit 0x01 ("failing now") first. Save the report, clear with 0x14 (allow for a slow 0x78 reply), run two ignition cycles and read again. A code that stays "failing now" with the factory connection restored points to a damaged connector or terminal. A disturbed radar would also need service-tool calibration, which is why this plan never touches the bumper.

The risks rank in this order. First, panda error frames on the radar link if the firmware is not patched or the patch regresses. Second, stub ringing from the long panda stub, which corrupts frames at the real nodes even with a silent panda. Third, an intermittent junction that causes mid-drive IMMEDIATE_DISABLEs. Fourth, damage to `C003B*6F`, which takes down all five radars. Fifth, a late-waking radar that the fingerprint misses. Sixth, a future change to `fisker.h` that stops rejecting the roughly 2,000 frames/s of bus-1 candidates, which would put them on the radar link.

## Conclusion

The wiring answer turned out simple and the safety answer did not. The drawings give one clear pair at one reachable connector, and comma's harness already reserves a pass-through pair for exactly this use. What the research exposed is that "receive only" in the port's code means "never sends frames", not "never drives the bus". The two differ by the ACK slot and the error flag, and on a 2 Mbit/s radar link that feeds ACC and AEB, the error flag is the dangerous one. A few lines of firmware close that gap, and they should ship before any wire is cut.

The open question that remains is physical: the panda's transceiver sits metres from the tap. The community capture proves the panda can hear the link that way. It does not prove that the radar and controller still hear each other cleanly with that stub attached. The scope at stage 10 and the controller's own fault flags decide whether this design stands or the listener has to move to the tap. Until both are clean, radar should stay out of the control loop, because a tap that fails partway through a drive is worse than no tap at all.
