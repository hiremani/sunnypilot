# Forward-only sensing caps the Ocean below FSD

**Short answer.** FSD (Supervised) v15 drives door to door. It does city turns, parking and reversing. It also yields to emergency vehicles and drives around potholes. It does this from a camera-only surround suite and one end-to-end network. This Fisker Ocean with a comma four and the `mallik-fisker` sunnypilot fork does much less. It keeps its lane well on the highway. It follows the car ahead. It can stop for mapped stop signs and for red lights its own camera sees. It can take slow, signalled junction turns from a map. Everything else is left to the driver. With all the software that is feasible, the car can get close to FSD on the highway and on forward-facing town tasks. It cannot get close on anything that needs to see beside or behind the car: merges, unprotected turns, roundabouts, gap choice for lane changes, and parking. That gap is in the sensors, not the code. On every one of 89 logged drives the radar link sent nothing, and the car's object list never once reported a car behind it. Note the date: **v15 began rolling out only on 21 Nov 2026**, five days before this report, and only to US Early Access owners on HW4. No independent v15 data exists yet. Where this report gives FSD performance numbers, they are for **v14.3**, and v14 data stands in for v15.

**How to read this report.** A *sourced* fact has a link. Web sources are linked by URL. Local sources are linked by path, where `SP/` means `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/`. Drive totals come from the comma's own drive summaries in `~/Fisker/routes/_summaries` (150 files, added up on 26 Nov 2026). Anything marked **Assessment** is this report's judgement. Tesla's statements are marked as Tesla claims, because nobody has checked them independently. Effort figures are all assessments.

## v15 is a better network, not a new feature list

Tesla describes v15.0.1 (software 2026.38.6.11) as an upgrade to "every part of the end-to-end driving network". It has a new vision encoder, shared with Robotaxi and Optimus. Tesla says this encoder improves "the critical intervention rate by 25x" ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/software-updates/version/2026.38.6.11/release-notes)). Tesla gives no baseline, no miles and no definition of "critical" for that number ([agentspulse](https://agentspulse.github.io/tutorials/tesla-fsd-v15/)). Musk says the driving model is **about 10x larger than v14 (about 10 billion parameters)** ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/4679/tesla-ai-chief-talks-about-whats-coming-in-fsd-v15)). Teslarati gives about 3 billion instead ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-rollout-begins-full-release-notes-improvements/)). The two figures do not agree. v15 runs only on HW4 (AI4). HW3 cars stay on "v14 Lite" ([agentspulse](https://agentspulse.github.io/tutorials/tesla-fsd-v15/)).

The new driving behaviours in v15 are few. The car now avoids **potholes and deep standing water**. It detects small hazards better: debris, chains, small animals and road signs ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-rollout-begins-full-release-notes-improvements/)). It slows for speed cameras. If you cancel the slowdown twice at the same camera, it stops slowing there. It has a Bike Rack Mode that pulls over instead of reversing. The network now picks the gear. It picks a parking spot from your own parking history ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/software-updates/version/2026.38.6.11/release-notes)). A learned model now plans the route, but only at the start and end of a trip ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-rollout-begins-full-release-notes-improvements/)). Most of what people mean by "FSD drives itself" came in v14 and carries into v15. That includes unprotected turns, gates, cut-ins, pulling over for emergency vehicles, school buses, detours with navigation fed into the network, and speed profiles from SLOTH to MAD MAX ([Not a Tesla App, v14.1](https://www.notateslaapp.com/software-updates/version/2025.32.8.16/release-notes)). v14.3 added parking, unparking, reversing and Start Self-Driving from Park. It also added about **20% faster reaction**, better compound traffic lights and better handling of emergency vehicles ([Not a Tesla App, v14.3](https://www.notateslaapp.com/software-updates/version/2026.38.5.10/release-notes)). The v15 notes say nothing about roundabouts, merges, pedestrians or night driving.

The best numbers we have are for v14. A crowdsourced tracker, analysed by Dominik Roth, shows how far FSD drives between critical disengagements. v13.2 managed **444 total miles (217 city)**. v14.3 managed **1,750 total miles (853 city)**. About 99.0% of v14.3 drives had no critical disengagement ([Roth](https://blog.dominik-roth.eu/posts/fsd-14.html)). Electrek works out that 25x would put v15 near 50,000 miles. That is an extrapolation, not a measurement ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-fsd-v15-rollout-25x-safety-reality-view/)). The first v15 reviews are from a few pro-Tesla accounts after a day or less of use. They report working pothole avoidance, better cornering and faster parking. They also report highway following that is too close and weak parking garages ([EVWire](https://evwire.com/p/tesla-fsd-v15-first-reviews)). Nobody has yet shown that v15 fixes the v14 failures regulators care about. These are railway crossings ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/08/27/tesla-fsd-likes-trains-too-much/)), red lights and wrong-lane entry (NHTSA PE25012, 80 incidents) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/feds-find-more-complaints-of-teslas-fsd-running-red-lights-and-crossing-lanes)), and losing the lead car in glare (NHTSA EA26002, 3.2M vehicles) ([CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/03/20/nhtsa-elevates-tesla-fsd-probe-to-engineering-analysis/)). Speed signs are also still read badly. A Brussels test found the car over the limit in 55% of 30 km/h zones ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-fsd-v15-rollout-25x-safety-reality-view/)). **Assessment:** expect a meaningful independent v15 number no earlier than Q1 2027. Discount early praise, because 14.1 to 14.2 showed a big first release followed by point-release regressions.

## This car drives from one forward camera

The Ocean's stack is openpilot's end-to-end driving model plus about ten assists written for the Fisker. These assists use map, profile and car signals. Steering is angle control. Steering works on its own (Stack A, the shipped default). In Stack A, speed comes from the car's own ACC ([interface.py](SP/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/fisker/interface.py)). Speed assists need openpilot longitudinal control (Stack B). Stack B needs a development flag and a debug panda firmware ([speed control plan](SP/reports/openpilot%20speed%20control%20plan.md)). Every custom assist is off by default in code. Many also have a shadow mode that only logs. The longitudinal planner takes the lowest speed target from all its sources on every tick. The sources include stop sign, red light, junction turn, speed bump, narrow street, crosswalk and wet road ([longitudinal_planner.py](SP/openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/controls/lib/longitudinal_planner.py)).

The sensor picture is the main fact. In all 510 sampled segments of 89 drives, the comma saw **0 frames at the radar's addresses**. `radarTracks` held 545,362 messages with 0 points. **All 216,510 lead frames were vision-only** ([phase0_log_measurements.md](SP/research_notes/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md)). The car's own object list never reported an object behind the car. That is 0 of 602,489 object frames. Its "beside" objects were all 0 to 5 m ahead of the bumper. Its range stopped at about 41 to 51 m. When the blind-spot flag was on, the list held a car on that side only 0.27% of the time. The corner radars do not feed the list ([phase0](SP/research_notes/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md)). The cameras are the comma's two forward road cameras and a cabin camera. The car's surround-view video is not tapped ([Reality View report](SP/reports/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility.md)). The comma four is also nearly full. CPU is p50 58% and max 90%. Memory is p50 68% and max 96%. The driving model takes 27.5 ms of its 50 ms budget ([phase0](SP/research_notes/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md)).

The evidence from 150 logged drives is mixed. Lateral control is the reliable part. openpilot steered for **946.5 of 1,379 moving minutes (about 69%)** over 689.5 miles. Speed control is still alpha quality. openpilot controlled speed for only **197.5 minutes**, in 582 runs. 280 of those runs ended with the driver on the gas, and 204 ended with openpilot turning off. openpilot's stops are rough: final-0.5 s jerk median **8.9 m/s³, against 1.4 for the driver's own stops** ([_summaries](~/Fisker/routes/_summaries)). The Map Turn Assist finished **117 of 165 junction turns (71%)**, and the driver overrode 32 (19%). Stop Sign Assist has run mostly in shadow: 466 shadow passes and 35 live passes. Red Light Assist has been checked only in replay. On 48 drives it engaged 159 times, and it would have braked 6 times when the driver drove on ([_summaries](~/Fisker/routes/_summaries); git fbbb744c0). Wet-road detection ran for 105.5 minutes. Its driving changes were applied for 0 minutes.

These numbers cannot be compared directly with FSD's miles per critical disengagement. The tracker counts only safety-critical takeovers. The summaries count every override and handover, including the planned ones. **Assessment:** even so, the supervision load is not close. FSD v14.3 drivers report a critical takeover every few hundred to few thousand miles. This car's 582 speed-control runs averaged about 20 seconds each before the driver or the system ended them.

Read the table by its columns. On the forward-facing rows the "software" column comes close to "Phase 4", which means software can do most of that work. Those rows are highway, curves, signs, town speed, stop signs and lights. On merges, unprotected turns, roundabouts, lane-change gaps and parking, the software column stays empty. **Assessment:** Phase 4 sensors make these rows *possible*, not *done*. A search of comma's posts and the main forks found no fusion of lidar, corner radars or surround cameras into planning. Forks use blind-spot data only as a yes/no flag on CAN ([arnepilot](https://github.com/arne182/openpilot); [FrogPilot](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot)). So each of those rows would be original work.

## Five limits no software update removes

**Perception is forward-only.** The car knows nothing beside or behind it except two blind-spot flags ([phase0](SP/research_notes/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md)). FSD's surround cameras give it the cross traffic and rear traffic that merges, unprotected turns and roundabouts need. Writing more code cannot replace that input.

**The car's actuation is deliberately gentle.** Acceleration is capped at **+1.0 m/s²**. Above that, on route 000000c6, the car's own plausibility check faulted. Braking is capped at −3.5 m/s². The wheel can turn at most **20°/s at 25 m/s**, and openpilot commands at most 400° of wheel angle ([values.py](SP/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/fisker/values.py)). openpilot's safety rules add a 3.0 m/s² lateral limit ([SAFETY.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/docs/SAFETY.md)). **Assessment:** these limits do not stop tight junction turns. A 10 m arc at 10 mph needs only about 2 m/s². They do stop FSD-style assertive merges, MAD MAX acceleration and quick evasive lane shifts. For hard emergencies the car must still rely on its own AEB.

**The comma four is full.** At about 58% CPU and 68% memory while driving, it has no room for a second perception network ([phase0](SP/research_notes/Tesla%20Reality%20View%20equivalent%20feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md)). comma's answer is chestnut, an external GPU. It costs **$249 bare or $799 with an RX 9060**, gives "similar compute to Tesla HW4", and is now where "our autonomy team will focus primarily" ([comma blog](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/)). **Assessment:** this makes chestnut the hardware most likely to matter in the long run. comma's best models will need it, and an on-device-only car will fall behind.

**openpilot does not follow a route.** comma removed Navigate on openpilot in 0.9.7. The model "could not reliably execute the maneuvers", and the feature has not come back ([comma blog 0.9.7](https://blog.comma.ai/097release/)). No fork steers through a junction on a navigation route ([FrogPilot](https://github.com/FrogAi/FrogPilot)). This car's map turn arc is already beyond what upstream does. Linking it to a route would be new ground.

**It is Level 2 for good.** openpilot is a "failsafe passive system" that needs an alert driver and "may disengage without warning" ([SAFETY.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/openpilot/master/docs/SAFETY.md)). Every command also needs the right SecOC key, and a wrong key silently blocks steering ([interface.py](SP/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/fisker/interface.py)). FSD is also supervised, but Tesla runs the same network without a monitor in eight Robotaxi cities ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Robotaxi)). The Ocean has no such path.

Three things are deliberately left off the list. The first is pothole avoidance. The driving model has no hazard output, and the comma has no room for a detector. The second is automatic creep and all-way-stop turn-taking. The car's own enhancements report advises against these, and no fork attempts them ([enhancements report](SP/reports/Fisker%20Ocean%20sunnypilot%20enhancements.md)). The third is any attempt at parking. That needs surround sensing or a safe tap into the car's APA, and both are high-risk unknowns. A map-based speed-camera slowdown, like v15's, is simple to build. **Assessment:** it adds little driving value and can wait.

## Conclusion

The gap to FSD is not spread evenly, and that changes what to do next. On the forward-facing tasks, software already closes most of the gap. The real problem there is quality: rough stops and speed control that drivers take back. On the surround tasks, the gap is total. It cannot be closed until something sees beside and behind the car. Even then, every fusion step would be original work. So the highest-value work for now is not to copy more FSD features. It is to make Stack B good enough to leave on, and to restore the forward radar the code already expects. After that, the car's own forward-facing abilities can run live instead of in shadow.

The more important point is about time. Tesla has turned driving into one large network on HW4-class compute, and v15 makes that network about 10x larger. comma has made the same move with chestnut and has said its future models will target it. **Assessment:** in a year, the main question for this car will not be which rules to add. It will be whether the comma four can still run the models worth having. The silent radar and the chestnut decision are therefore the two choices that set this car's ceiling. The ranked features above decide how close it gets to that ceiling. Revisit the FSD side of this comparison in January 2027, when the first independent v15 tracker data and structured tests should exist.
