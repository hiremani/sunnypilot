# Forward-only sensors cap a Reality View equivalent

A literal equivalent of Tesla's Reality View is not feasible on this setup, but a functional equivalent that is accurate ahead of the car and map-plausible elsewhere is, and most of it can be built with no new hardware. Reality View is not a 3D scene: it is real-time generated video from a roughly 1-billion-parameter model that reads the features of Tesla's surround-camera driving encoder and runs on half of one AI4 accelerator. The Fisker Ocean with a comma four has two forward road cameras, a driving model that publishes no objects, depth or occupancy, a comma with about 1.8 GB of free RAM, and a Pulse board whose NPU runs a 25-million-parameter transformer depth model at about 1.4 frames per second. The three ingredients Tesla's method needs (surround cameras, a strong shared encoder, a real-time video generator) are all absent, and no amount of software closes the first one. What the stack does have is unusually good for the alternative: Oceanscape already fuses a 32-slot radar, an 8-slot classified OEM object list and openpilot's leads into physically constrained tracks, draws them in three.js over OSM roads, and receives a frame-stamped 720p video stream with calibration. The recommendation is to build an "honest reality view" in four no-hardware phases (measure, enrich the twin with scenery and planner intent, project live camera pixels onto the road, add an on-Pulse detector), gate every phase on measured frame rate and comma load, and treat surround sensors, depth and Gaussian splats as later, separately gated options. Roughly 11 to 17 working weeks reach the end of the no-hardware path (Phases 0 to 3); the single largest risk is the Pulse's Mali GPU, which has no measured headroom while driving.

Local file citations use `SP/` for `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/sunnypilot/` and `OS/` for `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/oceanscape/`. Effort figures and success thresholds in the plan are this report's estimates, not sourced facts, and are marked as such.

## Reality View is generated video, not a rendered scene

Tesla's release notes describe "real-time generative driving visualization that continuously streams a view from an arbitrary virtual camera situated above your vehicle" ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-first-hand-look-at-reality-view-new-features/), [Not a Tesla App release notes](https://www.notateslaapp.com/software-updates/version/2026.38.6.11/release-notes)). It shipped on **2026-11-21 with FSD (Supervised) v15.0.1 in software 2026.38.6.11**, to Early Access owners on HW4/AI4 only, with wide release promised by Musk for December 2026 ([EVwire](https://evwire.com/p/tesla-fsd-v15-rollout-reality-view-release-notes)). All coverage is two days old and consists of first impressions; the primary X posts could not be opened directly, so every Tesla quote here comes through secondary outlets.

What the driver sees is a near-photoreal "drone shot" of the car and its real surroundings: buildings, sidewalks, people, grass, trees and signage, good enough that a first-hand reviewer found it hard to tell from a drone camera until garbled signage gave it away ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-first-hand-look-at-reality-view-new-features/), [Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/5065/tesla-fsd-v15-rolls-out-with-incredible-new-reality-view-and-25x-better-safety-video)). Planner intent is drawn in: chevrons for planned turns and pins for the chosen parking spot, which Musk described as re-rendering reality "with chevrons in the sky for turns" ([EVwire](https://evwire.com/p/tesla-fsd-v15-rollout-reality-view-release-notes)). The view moves to the front of the car in Reverse and returns in Drive, layout options are fullscreen, maps, split and a quiet mode, and second-hand reports describe tapping a point on the view to route toward it ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-first-hand-look-at-reality-view-new-features/), [Basenor](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/tesla-update-15-0-1)). Night, heavy rain, range, camera height and user pan/zoom are documented nowhere.

The mechanism, per Musk, is "real-time AI video generation (no gaming engine!) using just half of one AI4 TRIP accelerator" ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/23/tesla-fsd-v15-ai4-last-shot-compute-limit/)). The in-car model is a conditional mean-flow model of **about 1 billion parameters**, distilled from Tesla's large world model and conditioned on the recent sequence of features from the same "Universal Vision Encoder" that feeds the 3-billion-parameter driving model ([Musk on X](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124458557525397780), [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/23/tesla-fsd-v15-ai4-last-shot-compute-limit/)). It emits chunks of **8 images at 36 fps** at screen resolution, computed on every encoder frame at 18 Hz spread over 4 cycles, and each chunk starts from the last generated frame plus noise so the video stays continuous ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/5068/how-teslas-fsd-v15-reality-view-actually-works-video), [Not a Tesla Killer](https://notateslakiller.com/en/tesla-reality-view-fsd-v15-live-generated-screen-half-chip/)). Four cycles at 18 Hz is about 0.22 s of compute for 0.22 s of video, so the picture trails the world by at least one chunk plus encoder and display time; that figure is derived, not measured, and Musk concedes "the latency is a bit high" ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-first-hand-look-at-reality-view-new-features/)). No source confirms Gaussian splatting or explicit 3D reconstruction inside Tesla's pipeline. That idea comes from a YouTuber who rebuilt a similar view offline from 13 minutes of six-camera footage with about 3 hours of splat refinement, and for whom pure diffusion "failed" ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/5068/how-teslas-fsd-v15-reality-view-actually-works-video)).

Two consequences shape what "equivalent" should mean. First, the old Tesla visualization was a readout of detections, and Reality View is not: flat features such as traffic lights and crosswalks are now indistinguishable from plain video content, and Tesla is "clearly moving away from showing exactly what FSD sees" ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/5068/how-teslas-fsd-v15-reality-view-actually-works-video)). Second, its weak points are already visible: noticeable lag, textures that shift like early AI video, a "messy" look next to the old vector style, and wrong signage ([Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v15-first-hand-look-at-reality-view-new-features/), [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/23/tesla-fsd-v15-ai4-last-shot-compute-limit/)). An equivalent therefore has four defining properties: a camera-derived, realistic view of the real surroundings from a virtual third-person camera; live at video rate; planner intent overlaid; and no dependence on hand-built 3D assets. The Fisker setup can deliver the second and third fully, the first partially, and the fourth only in part.

## Five approaches, ranked by how close they get

The table scores each approach against those four properties. "Forward only" means the content is truthful only in the forward camera cone and for objects the radar and OEM list report.

| Approach | Real surroundings from a virtual camera | Live rate | Planner intent | No hand-built assets | Hardware cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Enriched object twin: existing fusion, map buildings, better models, intent overlays | Stylised; forward objects measured, sides and rear from map and memory | Yes, renderer interpolates 20 Hz data | Yes | No, relies on models | $0 | Build first |
| B. Camera pixels in the scene: live video projected onto the road surface, optional depth-displaced mesh | Real pixels on the forward ground; holes behind objects from a raised camera | Yes at 20 fps video | Yes | Partly | $0 | Build second, after a spike |
| C. On-Pulse detection feeding the twin: class, pedestrians, cones, lights | Stylised but far richer ahead | Detector 15-30 fps | Yes | No | $0 | Build third |
| D. Added surround sensing: cameras, lidar, or a Jetson | True 360-degree content for the first time | Yes | Yes | Depends on renderer | $399 to about $1,400 | Decide after A-C |
| E. Tesla's method: generative video, or live Gaussian splats | Photoreal in principle | Not on this compute | Yes | Yes | External GPU, training data that does not exist | Research only |

Approach A is an extension of what already runs. Automaker-style object twins need tens of thousands of triangles, no per-frame sorting and plain WebGL2, which makes them the lowest-risk rendering choice on a Mali-G610; that ranking is an assessment from the research, not a benchmark. Its ceiling is honest stylisation: a clean scene that is correct ahead and plausible elsewhere, comparable to pre-v15 automaker visualizations.

Approach B is the only route to real pixels without new hardware, and its limits are geometric. Camera pixels can be re-projected faithfully only near the camera's own viewpoint; from a raised virtual camera a depth-reprojected image shows holes behind every object and "rubber sheet" stretching at depth edges. The road surface is the exception, because it is close to a plane: projecting the calibrated video onto the ground and keeping what the camera saw as the car drives over it gives real asphalt, paint, cracks and wet patches under and behind the car, with tracked objects drawn as models on top. This specific design is this report's synthesis and needs a spike; the bridge already sends the intrinsics and projection matrix it requires (`SP/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/video.py:74-104`).

Approach C is where the Pulse's idle NPU pays off. Rockchip's model-zoo figures put **yolov8n at 73.5 FPS and yolov8n-seg at 60.8 FPS** on one RK3588 NPU core at 640x640 INT8, inference only ([Seeed SenseCraft benchmarks](https://sensecraft.seeed.cc/ai-lab/en/tutorials/rk/benchmark/cv)). The same NPU is weak on transformers: the only published figure for Depth Anything V2 Small is **about 696 ms per 518x518 frame**, unquantised on three cores, from a single self-published card ([RK3588 model card](https://huggingface.co/anguoyang/depth-anything-v2-small-rknn-rk3588)). Detection and segmentation fit; dense depth at frame rate does not, though depth at 1-2 Hz for slowly changing static geometry remains an option because the renderer can extrapolate between updates.

Approach D is the only one that closes the surround gap. The published feed-forward reconstruction methods that could synthesise unseen views all assume surround rigs: FlashOcc is trained on six-camera nuScenes data and benchmarked on an RTX 3090 ([FlashOCC](https://github.com/Yzichen/FlashOCC)), DrivingForward takes "flexible surround-view input" ([DrivingForward](https://github.com/fangzhou2000/DrivingForward)), and STORM has no official checkpoint ([GaussianSTORM](https://github.com/NVlabs/GaussianSTORM)). None transfers to one forward camera without retraining on data nobody has published.

Approach E is out of reach for structural reasons, not tuning ones. comma's own world model is a 2-billion-parameter diffusion transformer that samples at 12.2 frames per second per data-centre GPU and never runs on the device ([comma blog, 0.11](https://blog.comma.ai/011release/)). The on-device driving model is about 1.7 GFLOPs ([comma blog, 0.11.2](https://blog.comma.ai/0112release/)), its latents have no published decoder, and there is no surround encoder to condition on. Splat rendering is unmeasured on any Mali-G610; the published mobile baseline is an iPhone 13 Pro Max at 38 fps in WebGL2 for 1 million splats ([PlayCanvas](https://blog.playcanvas.com/new-in-supersplat-webgpu-and-streaming-bring-huge-performance-wins)), and a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 compute pipeline spends 267-506 ms per frame on sorting alone ([arXiv 2511.16298](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16298v1)). A pre-built static splat of a frequently driven route, with live objects on top, is a credible later experiment, but no project doing it for a car display was found.

## The stack already holds the transport, the tracker and the twin

The data and plumbing an equivalent needs are largely in place, which is why the first phases cost no hardware. Every public openpilot visualization found (opview, CommaView, T-UI, op-remote-ui) relocates the 2D camera-plus-overlay screen; the one 3D "reality view" located is Oceanscape itself, in this repository, and it gets its surrounding cars from the vehicle's own buses, not from the comma model ([moistpope/oceanfront-view](https://github.com/moistpope/oceanfront-view), [eFiniLan/opview](https://github.com/eFiniLan/opview)).

**Objects.** The HUD receives a 32-slot mid-range radar cycle every 65 ms with range, lateral position, relative velocity, heading, width, length and class (`SP/opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/fisker/radar_interface.py:6-11`, `OS/dbc/fisker_ocean_mrr.dbc:82-117`); an 8-slot OEM camera object list with position, size, height and nine classes out to about 50 m (`OS/INTERNALS.md:474`, `OS/dbc/fisker_ocean_adas_world.dbc:867-879`); and openpilot's two leads. A web worker fuses them into ground-fixed tracks, each an extended Kalman filter over a kinematic bicycle with class-based physical limits, then draws simulated bodies steered toward the estimate. Against the tracker it replaced on the demo drive, overlapping vehicle pairs fell **from 174 to 0** and 95th-percentile jitter from 1.6 to 1.0 cm (`OS/INTERNALS.md:172-205`). The tracker is not the limiting factor; its inputs are.

Correction from the 2026-11-26 log measurements: in all 89 recorded drives the radar's link carries nothing, so the radar input above exists in the code and in one 61 s capture, not in practice. On the drives on record the forward objects come from the OEM list and openpilot's vision leads only, and nothing reports what is beside or behind the car except the two blind-spot flags.

**Road and intent.** openpilot's `modelV2` publishes the planned path, up to 4 lane lines and 2 road edges as 33 points out to 192 m with x, y and z, plus lane-change state, a `shouldStop` flag and desired curvature and acceleration, all at 20 Hz ([cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp), [modeld constants](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/selfdrive/modeld/constants.py)). That is everything needed for Tesla-style intent overlays, and the bridge already forwards the path, lanes, edges, lane-change state and the longitudinal plan (`SP/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/extract.py:15-23,155-191`). The OEM camera adds six lane lines, stop-line and crosswalk distances, the ego-lane traffic light state and speed-limit signs (`OS/INTERNALS.md:473,477`).

**Video.** The fork ships an onroad video path that stock openpilot lacks: the livestream encoder's H.264 at **1280x720, 20 fps, 5 Mbit/s, keyframe every 5 frames**, passed untouched over a WebSocket with a per-frame header carrying frame id and timestamp, the camera intrinsics and calibrated projection, and the model output matched by frame id (`SP/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/video.py:10-24,74-135`, `SP/openpilot/system/loggerd/loggerd.h:17,29-40`). Road and wide cameras are selectable. The page decodes with WebCodecs and draws to a 2D canvas window (`OS/static/js/roadcam.js:1-7,41`).

**Map and pose.** Road geometry comes from sunnypilot mapd's OSM tiles, point features (signals, stop and give-way signs, crossings) from Overpass per 0.05 degree cell, prefetched within 25 km and kept on disk (`OS/static/js/world/osmtile.js:1-14`, `OS/INTERNALS.md:435-441`). Pose is dead-reckoned from wheel speed and yaw rate and corrected by 10 Hz GPS (`OS/INTERNALS.md:317-326`). Building footprints are one more query in a pipeline that already fetches and caches per-cell features, and OSM-derived data is ODbL, which permits offline storage with attribution; Google's Photorealistic 3D Tiles prohibit pre-fetching, caching and offline use and are ruled out ([Overture buildings guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/buildings), [Google Map Tiles API policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies?authuser=1)).

**Rendering and platform.** The scene is three.js r160 on WebGL with a true-size rigged ego model, procedural low-poly road users "in the soft, matte style of Tesla's visualization", lanes, junctions, street furniture and a route ribbon (`OS/static/js/models.js:1-2`, `OS/INTERNALS.md:360-390`). An auto-resolution scaler and an on-screen counter for fps, draw calls and triangles already exist (`OS/static/js/perf.js:9-17,101-112`). Measured in this session: the Pulse is an **RK3588S** (`rk3588s_t`, model EVPlay) on Android 13, a userdebug build with `su`, driving a 1080x1920 portrait display; the HUD's own counter read 49 fps once, parked, top-down, with no traffic. The Android app already ships and executes a native helper without root (`OS/android/README.md:128-131`), so a native inference service has a precedent. `tools/replay` plus recorded routes gives a full offline development loop without the car ([comma docs](https://docs.comma.ai/tools/replay/README.html)).

## Every gap, from missing cameras to missing measurements

The shortfalls fall into five groups. The first two tables list what is structurally absent; the third lists what nobody has measured, and those unknowns decide the plan more than the known limits do.

### Sensors and data

| Gap | Evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| No side or rear cameras | Only wide and narrow road cameras plus a cabin camera exist; the factory surround-view video is not tapped and only its status label is on CAN (session measurement; `OS/static/js/world/fisker_world.js:540`) | A 360-degree camera-derived view is impossible; "360° vision" in comma's marketing counts the cabin camera |
| No side or rear object source beyond flags | Radar origin is the front bumper with unsigned range; blind spot is two booleans plus a threat ID; corner radars sit on private links (`OS/static/js/world/fisker_radar.js:11-12`, `SP/openpilot/sunnypilot/webhud/extract.py:59-60`) | Traffic alongside and behind is memory, a flag, or absent. Measured: the OEM list never reports an object behind, drops a car as it comes alongside, and holds the blind-spot car in only 0.27% of flagged frames |
| No depth, occupancy or general objects from the driving model | `ModelDataV2` has no field for objects, classes, boxes, depth, segmentation or occupancy ([cereal/log.capnp](https://github.com/commaai/openpilot/blob/master/cereal/log.capnp)) | Nothing fills the scene beyond road shape and one or two leads without added perception |

With no radar data, distant objects are absent rather than unclassified |

| No elevation from radar | Overhead lights, gantries and bridges are tracked as stopped cars until passed (`OS/INTERNALS.md:225-229`) | False stationary vehicles unless a camera check suppresses them |

| Route intent is thin | The route is rebuilt from the nav app's next-step notification only (`OS/INTERNALS.md:395-402`) | Turn chevrons are reliable one step ahead, not for a full route |
| No training data or generator | commaVQ and comma10k are forward-camera datasets; no depth dataset from comma was found ([commaVQ](https://github.com/commaai/commavq), [comma10k](https://www.github.com/commaai/comma10k)) | No path to a learned novel-view model for this rig |

## A phased plan that spends nothing until Phase 4

The plan orders work so that every phase ships a visible improvement and no hardware is bought before the cheap measurements have said whether it is needed. Efforts are estimates for one developer; success thresholds are proposed targets.

| Phase | Goal | Effort (est.) | Hardware | Gate to proceed |

### Phase 0: two weeks of measurements decide everything after

The goal is to turn the unknowns table into numbers and to write down what "equivalent" must mean on this car. All log analysis runs on the Mac after syncing logs, never on the comma. From existing drive logs, measure how often the OEM object list reports objects with negative longitudinal distance and at what range, the radar's real range and track counts, `modelV2.modelExecutionTime` and frame-drop percentage, and whether leads are radar-backed. On the comma, record `procLog` and `deviceState` for one drive with the camera view on and one with it off, and test whether road and wide streams can be encoded together without drops. On the Pulse, record the WebView version, the WebGL and WebGPU contexts and GPU driver, the `perf.js` counters over a full drive, hardware-decode status, glass-to-glass video latency, and RAM and temperature; remove or stop the three other HUD apps first, since the idle load average of 6-8 distorts every reading. Run four spikes: a video-frame-to-WebGL-texture upload timing; a native RKNN yolov8n-seg on decoded 720p frames with pre- and post-processing in native code; a Depth Anything V2 Small conversion at INT8 and roughly 256-384 px; and a 100k-500k splat scene in a WebGL2 viewer. Finally, dump the driving model's ONNX output slices to confirm nothing useful is discarded before `modelV2`.

Status 2026-11-26: the log-only measurements are done (`research_notes/Tesla Reality View equivalent feasibility/phase0_log_measurements.md`). They settle three things. The OEM list sees nothing behind the car. The radar sends nothing to the comma on any recorded drive. openpilot's leads are vision-only. They also give the video-off baseline the gate compares against: model execution p99 30.6 ms of a 50 ms frame, frames dropped in 2.3% of model frames, device CPU p95 68%, memory up to 96%. What is left needs the car: one drive with the camera view on (the comma side of the video gate), and the Pulse measurements, which the HUD's drive logger (v1.7.0 and later) now records on every drive. Add one cheap item: find out why the radar link is silent (harness, connector or the radar itself) before any design leans on radar.

The decision gate is strict. If the Pulse cannot hold about 30 fps in traffic with today's scene, Phase 1 begins with renderer optimisation (instancing, LOD, cheaper ground) and buildings wait. If video-on raises comma frame drops or pushes plannerd toward its 50 ms budget, video stays an opt-in window and Phase 2 is dropped. If the splat spike fails, Phase 5 closes.

### Phase 1: planner intent and scenery on the existing twin

The goal is a view that reads like Reality View's composition, a chase camera above and behind the car with the planned manoeuvre drawn in, built only from data already on the wire. The work items are a "drone" camera preset with a Reverse-gear flip to the front, matching Tesla's behaviour; intent overlays from `modelV2` (the planned path as a ribbon, lane-change direction, stop intent from `shouldStop` and the longitudinal plan, turn chevrons from the next navigation step); OSM building footprints fetched through the existing Overpass cache and extruded at load time; instancing and level of detail in the renderer so buildings and traffic do not add draw calls linearly; a small set of better vehicle models by class; time-of-day lighting from clock and GPS; and provenance styling, with full opacity for live-measured objects, ghosting for dead-reckoned ones and a neutral style for map-only geometry. A view that draws remembered cars as solid will show cars that are no longer there, so the ghosting is a correctness feature, not decoration. A lower, forward-biased camera option hides the unobserved area behind the car and should be the default if the rear turns out to be empty.

Risks are GPU budget (mitigated by the existing auto-scaler and the Phase 0 numbers), map misalignment of a few metres (mitigated by snapping the map to camera lane lines, as the code already does for OS data), and the route source being one step deep. Success means the fps floor holds with buildings on, intent overlays match what the car then does on recorded drives, and no ghost object stays solid for more than about a second after its last measurement.

### Phase 2: real camera pixels on the road surface

The goal is the first "reality" in the view: the actual road, with its paint, patches and wet reflections, under the stylised objects. The work items are to route decoded video frames into a WebGL texture, project them onto the ground plane using the calibration the bridge already sends, blend the wide camera near the car with the narrow camera farther out, accumulate a rolling ground texture in the ground-fixed frame so the road under and behind the car keeps what the camera saw, and mask the pixels where tracked objects stand so their smear does not paint the road. A sky and a soft horizon blend cover the region the projection cannot reach.

The main risks are video-texture cost in this WebView, which is undocumented for Android, and latency. The native fallback, a hardware-decoded surface composited behind a transparent WebView, avoids the page entirely but cannot be warped per pixel, so it only serves a picture-in-picture mode. Tesla's own view trails reality by at least about 0.22 s by derivation, so a projected-video latency under 200 ms would match or beat it; the published reference for a no-inference camera-to-display pipeline is TI's 60-65 ms ([TI SPRADC4](https://ti.com/lit/spradc4)). Success means the projected road stays registered to the lane lines within a lane-marking width at 30 m and the renderer keeps its floor.

### Phase 3: a detector on the Pulse's idle NPU

The goal is to populate the forward scene with what neither the radar nor the 8-slot list reports well: pedestrians, cyclists, cones, vehicle type and colour, traffic lights and signs across the junction, and a camera veto for the radar's overhead-structure ghosts. The work items are a native Android service that links the RKNN runtime, takes frames from the hardware decoder, does resize and colour conversion on the Rockchip RGA path and non-maximum suppression in native code, and pushes detections to the page over a loopback socket; a fourth measurement source in the world model that lifts detections to the ground plane using the calibration and associates them with radar range; sampled vehicle colour applied to the drawn models; and a comma10k-trained small segmenter as a later option for drivable-surface masks, since comma10k is MIT-licensed and matches the camera ([comma10k](https://www.github.com/commaai/comma10k)). Ultralytics documents a direct YOLO-to-RKNN export ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/rockchip-rknn/)); check the model licence before anything is distributed, and prefer the permissively licensed detectors in Rockchip's zoo if it will be.

Risks are RKNN bring-up on this vendor Android image, which no source documents, low pixel density on distant objects, and CPU contention with decoding and rendering. An assembled budget from published components puts object latency at roughly 100-150 ms, hidden by the tracker extrapolating at display rate. Success means 15 fps or better end to end, measurably fewer ghost vehicles under bridges, and pedestrians appearing in the view on replayed town drives where they were previously absent.

The recommended first purchase, if any, is the cheapest one that answers the open question. Phase 0 answered it: the OEM object list does not report neighbours or followers (no object behind in 602,000 object frames, and the blind-spot car in only 0.27% of flagged frames). So any real content beside or behind the car needs added sensing; a Jetson alone would add depth ahead but nothing behind. Before buying anything, restore the radar link, which costs nothing and brings back forward range beyond the list's ~51 m. After that, a single 360-degree lidar is the shortest route to true surround geometry, because it sidesteps both the missing cameras and the missing depth capability; two corner radar modules are the cheaper route if only moving cars beside and behind matter; surround cameras on the Pulse remain the most labour for the least certain result. The decision gate is the driver's own judgement after a few weeks with Phase 3: buy only if the empty or ghosted rear is what actually bothers.

### Phase 5: a research track that stays off the car

Two experiments are worth running on the Mac without commitment. The first is an offline splat or mesh of one frequently driven route built from recorded forward video, loaded as static scenery under the live objects; the YouTuber's six-camera rebuild shows the method works offline, and a forward-only version will be weaker to the sides. It proceeds to the car only if the Phase 0 splat spike held 30 fps. The second is watching comma's model releases: if a future driving model exposes richer outputs or documented latents, the conditioning problem changes. Neither belongs on a schedule.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that Tesla traded one thing for another, and this car should not make the same trade. Reality View gave up the honest readout of what the system detected in exchange for a picture that looks real, and its early critics already miss the readout. The Fisker stack cannot generate that picture, but it can keep the readout and add reality where reality is actually observed: real pixels on the road the camera sees, measured objects ahead, map geometry labelled as map, and memory labelled as memory. That is a different product from Tesla's, arguably a more trustworthy one for a supervised system, and it is one that lags reality by less than Tesla's derived 0.2 s floor.

The plan's real dependency is not perception but two unglamorous numbers nobody has: the Pulse's frame rate in traffic and the comma's load with video on. They settle whether the remaining work is spent adding realism or first buying back GPU headroom. The only hardware question is now answered from the logs: the car's own object list does not see behind it, so a rear view needs added sensing, and the radar link should be fixed first. Nothing should be bought, and no neural network added, until those numbers exist.
