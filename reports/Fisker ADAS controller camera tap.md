# Prove the tap on salvage first

A passive tap on the Fisker Ocean's four surround-camera links is feasible on paper but unproven anywhere in public, so the right move is a bench study on salvage parts now and no in-car fitting until that bench work passes; for a third-party display that is needed soon, fit independent aftermarket cameras (about $250 to $500) instead. The controller ("Hydra3", Fisker part FM2980140120B) sits behind the lower side trim of the rear cargo area, and all four links arrive on one blue quad Rosenberger HFM connector, `C003B*4F`, in the order right, left, rear, front. The link is very probably TI FPD-Link III carrying unencrypted RAW Bayer video from onsemi AR0233AT sensors with power fed up the coax, but only the sensor is confirmed; the chips, format, frame rate and coax voltage are not. The one commercial listen-only device that fits, TZ's quad HFM coupler CPL0710, has no published price or loss figure, and it passes all four links through one box, so a fault there puts the rear camera and Reverse Automatic Emergency Braking (RAEB) at stake along with the park features. The car checks each link every 100 ms and sets a code after 500 ms of lost lock or frozen image, with a CRC criterion that has no stated threshold, and nobody has documented whether those codes heal on their own. The tap's output would be raw sensor data that needs a full software ISP, not a ready picture. Nothing in this report has been tested on a car: the documents are community copies of 2023-era Fisker material, and every procedure step is a proposal.

## The controller is in the cargo area, and one blue quad connector carries all four links

### Location and access

The service manual's "Control Unit - ADAS" procedure places the unit in the **rear compartment behind the lower side trim**, not behind the dash. The steps are: disconnect the 12 V battery, remove the rear compartment lower trim, remove eight M6x20 Torx screws (5 Nm) and the trim mounting bracket, disconnect **six harness connectors**, and remove three M6 nuts (9 Nm) ([Service manual p.80-1, PDF p.237](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1BwRtG41yIPEU5H6KvpWaj1X8OWSvLYuf/view)). The lower trim itself comes off only after the upper trim, the compartment lamp, the carpet, the scuff plate (three bolts, eight clips) and the partition box ([Service manual p.40-29](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1BwRtG41yIPEU5H6KvpWaj1X8OWSvLYuf/view)). **The side is uncertain**: the step says "RH" trim but refers to the "LH" trim procedure, and the manual's illustrations were not viewed. Check both sides before removing trim. Two cues help: the unit's fuse, MF07 (Micro, 15 A, "ADAS DC"), is in the rear trunk fuse box ([fuses.json](https://fisker-ocean-archive.preetham-981.workers.dev/data/fuses.json)), and owners report a continuously running "ADAS fan" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sXyRU3yCyog)), which suggests the unit has forced cooling. Its bracket (FM2980140040A) is noted as also supporting another unit, probably the liftgate module, so expect a crowded space ([Parts book](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1j-PSGpu0bNz-jYVWydXP8Ga0_e9ujeEU/view)).

The manual calls for programming and calibration with the Fisker Aftersales Service Tool after the unit is *replaced*. Unplugging and re-plugging the same unit should not need that, but this is an assumption; the manual does not say. The cargo-area location is good for a tap because all four links end at one place near a 12 V fuse box. Its cost is distance: the tapped video must still travel forward to wherever the display is.

### The six connectors and the camera map

Wiring sheet VII.4 of the MY2023 set shows six connectors, which agrees with the manual's count ([Wiring set p.28, p.51](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SY0NpmfMqZCx2eLu4gMEKs2z0kN_mZQT/view)).

| Connector | Part code | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `C003B*1F` | 34729-0200 | Supply, ground, ADAS CAN, IBUS2 CAN, ultrasonic power and DSI pairs |
| `C003B*2F` | E6K10A-1CAZ5-C | Ethernet pair to the front camera module |
| `C003B*3F` | AMK12A-1M4Z5-B | `DIG_OUT_LVDS` coax to the ICC head unit |
| **`C003B*4F`** | **AMK12D-1M4Z5-C** | **Four surround coax links, positions `D*01` to `D*04`, each with a drain** |
| `C003B*5F` | AMK12D-1M4Z5-D | Driver-monitoring camera coax |
| `C003B*6F` | 560123-0200 | Private CAN pairs for five radars, driver-camera LED lines |

On left-hand-drive cars the positions on `C003B*4F` are **`D*01` right, `D*02` left, `D*03` rear, `D*04` front**. The fault-code text gives the same order for the controller's camera power channels 1 to 4, with the driver camera on channel 5 of a second bank ([dtc.json](https://fisker-ocean-archive.preetham-981.workers.dev/data/dtc.json)). Right-hand-drive cars use separate wires that swap `D*01` and `D*02`, so do not label channels by position alone. Two limits apply to this map. No sheet shows which physical cavity of the housing is `D*01`, and the map comes from design documents, not from a check on a car. Confirm it on the bench by tracing a salvage harness tail, or in the car by matching one channel to one view.

The cable routes set the signal margin. The front camera link is the longest and crosses two inline joints (`I136A`, `I141B`); the rear crosses one (`I147D`); each side link crosses one at the door (`I151J` left, `I152K` right) ([Wiring set p.28](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SY0NpmfMqZCx2eLu4gMEKs2z0kN_mZQT/view)). The front link therefore has the least margin for added loss.

### The connector itself

Rosenberger's datasheet for AMK12D-1M4Z5 confirms an **HFM straight jack with quad housing, coding C (blue)**: 50 ohm, DC to 15 GHz, return loss at least 25 dB to 3 GHz, insertion loss up to 0.1 dB per GHz, up to 1 A DC, and **at least 25 mating cycles** ([Rosenberger datasheet](https://products.rosenberger.com/_ocassets/db/AMK12D-1M4Z5-Y.pdf)). The connector is not the weak point of this link; the tap and its extra joints are. The mating-cycle figure is a real budget, since every trial that opens `C003B*4F` uses one. The driver-camera connector is coding D (bordeaux violet), so the two quad plugs cannot be swapped by mistake. Because the harness part is a jack, the controller's header is most likely a C-coded quad plug; an in-line tap needs a C-coded plug facing the harness and a C-coded jack facing the controller. That is inferred from the harness side only, and the header's part number was not found.

**The fault text reveals more of the architecture than the teardown summary does.** The controller has a deserializer with a lock status per camera, reads camera registers over I2C (the sensor temperature and a CRC), and switches camera power per channel with open-circuit and short detection, in two banks: `CAM_CS_A` for the four surround cameras and `CAM_CS_B` for the driver camera ([dtc.json](https://fisker-ocean-archive.preetham-981.workers.dev/data/dtc.json)). Three consequences follow. First, the controller itself configures and polls the bare sensor through the link's back channel, so a tap must leave the back channel and the DC path fully intact. Second, five coax inputs exceed one quad hub, so a second deserializer probably serves the driver camera; the TechInsights public list is partial. Third, the power-switch pin pattern (IN, Fault, current sense) matches a quad smart high-side switch such as TI's TPS4H160-Q1 ([TI datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps4h160-q1.pdf)), which detects open load and short to battery. That is the mechanism by which a tap that disturbs camera supply current would be caught.

**RAW output is the working assumption for three reasons.** The AR0233AT has no on-chip ISP and is sold for use with companion or third-party ISPs ([onsemi brief](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/7169/AR0233AT-3688810.pdf)). The controller reads the sensor's registers directly. And TI's own reference camera pairs the AR0233 with a DS90UB953 on one FAKRA coax, sends RAW12 to RAW24 formats, takes 4 V to 18.3 V over the coax (12 V nominal), and was tested at 1080p60 into a DS90UB960 ([TI TIDA-050035](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/tiduev5/tiduev5.pdf)). That design is the closest public analogue of this link. A camera-side ISP with YUV output is not excluded, because no camera teardown exists.

On that family, the forward channel runs at about 4 Gbps (160 times the deserializer's 25 MHz reference, Nyquist near 2.1 GHz) and the back channel at 50 Mbps in synchronous mode ([TI DS90UB960-Q1 datasheet, section 7.4.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ds90ub960-q1.pdf)). These numbers frame everything a tap must respect, but they apply only if the best-guess parts are the fitted parts. **Reading the chip markings on a salvage controller and a salvage camera settles the family, the mode and the HDCP question in one step**, and measuring DC on a powered salvage coax settles the coupler variant. No sibling vehicle using this controller was found, so there is no shortcut through another car's documentation.

## A directional coupler keeps the OEM link whole, and a silenced deserializer listens

### What the tap may cost the link

TI publishes a hard budget for this link class: the equalizer compensates up to **19.2 dB of channel loss at 2.1 GHz**, the channel must be free of resonances below Nyquist, total-channel return loss must stay better than 9 dB from 1 to 2.1 GHz, and a 6 to 15 m run may contain at most four in-line connectors ([TI channel requirements](https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/communityserver-discussions-components-files/138/Channel_2D00_Requirements_2D00_ADAS-chipsets-FPD-III.pdf); [DS90UB960-Q1 datasheet, section 7.4.7.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ds90ub960-q1.pdf)). TI's worked example budgets 0.1 dB per connector and quotes Dacar 462 coax at about 1.31 dB/m at 2.1 GHz. The power-over-coax network must stay high-impedance from 25 MHz to 2.1 GHz with under 1.75 ohm of DC resistance.

Against that budget, a tap is affordable only if its through-loss is a fraction of a dB. Textbook arithmetic gives the ideal through-loss of a directional coupler as about 0.46 dB for a 10 dB coupling factor and 0.14 dB for 15 dB. Adding two connector interfaces, a 10 dB coupler should cost roughly 0.7 to 1 dB at 2.1 GHz, equal to about 0.5 to 0.75 m of extra cable, plus one or two joints against the limit of four. The rear and side links have room for that by route length; the front link, already at two inline joints, has the least. These are estimates, not measurements, and **how much margin each Ocean link actually has is unknown**.

Three tap types exist, and only one suits a car that is driven. A **directional coupler** leaves the OEM link electrically end to end, isolates its coupled port from DC, and, when oriented toward the camera, suppresses the controller's back-channel signal by its directivity. That matters because the original deserializer cancels its own back-channel echo, while a listener sees a foreign back channel at 25 to 50 MHz inside the forward band and cannot subtract it. A **resistive pick-off** (for example 450 ohm into 50 ohm, about 0.42 dB through-loss in the ideal case) is not directional, needs a stub of millimetres and a DC block, and delivers both directions superimposed; a high-pass above 50 MHz on the tap arm would help. An **active in-line device** terminates the camera link and regenerates a new one toward the controller, so the OEM link then depends on the added box's power, firmware and PoC forwarding. That last class is rejected for road use.

### What hardware exists

**TZ Electronic Systems is the only vendor confirmed to sell a passive coupler for these links.** Its flyer maps the models: CPL0610 is a single HFM coupler for lines with power over coax, **CPL0710 is the four-channel HFM version with PoC**, and CPL0615 and CPL0715 are the non-PoC versions, which are unsuitable on a powered line ([TZ coupler flyer](https://tz-es.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/TZ_Product_Flyer_Coupler.pdf)). The PoC models route DC through the main path and present the signal without DC at the forward output. The single-channel units measure 64 x 102.9 x 40 mm and need no supply. TZ publishes **no insertion loss, coupling factor, directivity, PoC current rating, temperature range or price**; the flyer's "lossless" is a marketing word. Whether the CPL0710's connector gender and coding C match `C003B*4F`, and whether TZ sells single units to individuals, need TZ's written answer.

Everything else sold as a "tap" is the active kind. The b-plus MDILink GMSL2 TAP is a deserializer plus serializer with an FPGA between them, 15 to 25 W and 0.8 kg, and it is for GMSL2 ([b-plus datasheet](https://www.b-plus.com/fileadmin/data_storage/Data_Storage/1_Main_Navigation/1_Portfolio/04_Datenverteilung_und_Konvertierung/MDILink/Datenblaetter/Datasheet_MDILink_GMSL2_TAP-1.pdf)). Xylon's FPD-Link III module with "tap mode" is a DS90UB953 plus DS90UB954 pair, so it is almost certainly in-line as well ([Xylon](https://xylon-lab.com/product-category/i-o-modules/video-modules/)). None publishes a price. A do-it-yourself coupler is conceivable (a wideband 50 ohm coupler covering roughly 20 MHz to 3 GHz), but general RF couplers are not rated to pass hundreds of milliamps of DC, so it would need a purpose-built board; no specific part was verified.

### Listening without joining the link

The DS90UB960 datasheet supports receive-only operation without naming it. Forward-channel lock, input frequency (registers 0x4F/0x50), parity errors, line count and line length are all local reads that need no reply from the serializer, and the receiver simply recovers the clock set by the car's own deserializer ([DS90UB960-Q1 datasheet, section 7.4.9](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ds90ub960-q1.pdf)). TZ confirms the principle from the vendor side: a second deserializer on the coupler's forward output "cannot communicate with the serializer" and "must be specially configured", which TZ calls "coupler mode" ([TZ coupler flyer](https://tz-es.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/TZ_Product_Flyer_Coupler.pdf)). No register sequence for that mode is public, and no TI document found says a second receiver is supported.

**The specific hazard is that a TI deserializer transmits by default.** Bit `BC_ALWAYS_ON` in register 0x58 resets to 1, so a DS90UB954 or DS90UB960 drives a back channel on its input pins from power-up until firmware clears it, and the back-channel rate field has no "off" code ([DS90UB960-Q1 datasheet, Table 7-98](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ds90ub960-q1.pdf)). Through a coupler that signal travels toward the camera, attenuated only by the coupling factor, on top of the controller's real back channel. A TI forum thread on disabling the back channel is reported to cite two different bits in different answers ([TI E2E](https://e2e.ti.com/support/interface-group/interface/f/interface-forum/703711/ds90ub960-q1-back-channnel-disable)), which is a reason to verify silence with a scope on the bench. The defences, strongest first, are a one-way RF buffer on the tap arm (the only one that survives a firmware crash), a coupler with good directivity plus an attenuator, and a first I2C write that clears 0x58 while the port is held disabled in register 0x0C. The sniffer board must also have no PoC supply fitted, and a stock camera-kit deserializer must never touch a live coax.

The unknown link settings are recoverable without talking to the camera. There are only four receive modes to try (CSI-2, RAW10, RAW12-HF, RAW12-LF); the frequency register gives the rate; in CSI-2 mode the camera's own packet headers pass through, so the data type and virtual channel are visible to the host. The tap signal is weaker and flatter than a cable signal, so the adaptive equalizer should be pinned to a narrow range found on the bench. One real unknown is receiver sensitivity: no source gives the minimum input amplitude, so a 10 to 20 dB coupling factor might leave too little signal. TZ's older couplers were actively amplified, probably for this reason.

No public precedent exists. No write-up, repository or paper was found in which anyone passively tapped an OEM camera link in a production car and recovered video; the nearest trace is an unanswered November 2026 request on Hyundai/Kia surround cameras ([hmg_nemesis issue 85](https://github.com/xwtk/hmg_nemesis/issues/85)). The practice clearly exists inside OEM validation, which is TZ's stated market, but anyone doing this on an Ocean should expect to be first to publish.

## The car checks each link every 100 ms, and the consequences are documented

### Fault codes and what they cost

Each surround camera has one "Component Failure" code in the ADAS controller. All four use the same eight criteria: power-channel open circuit or short to battery for 500 ms; short to ground for 500 ms; sensor temperature above 125 deg C for 500 ms; **"Lock failure on deserializer side for 500 ms"**; **"Not receiving any image, images are frozen, images are not being refreshed for 500ms"**; **"CRC Error (Read thru register over I2C)" with no time or count threshold**; calibration data not configured or alignment incorrect; and calibration data unreadable from flash. The monitor runs continuously at 100 ms ([dtc.json](https://fisker-ocean-archive.preetham-981.workers.dev/data/dtc.json); [DTC manual, PDF sheets 305 to 309](https://drive.google.com/file/d/17oA-uFKGrGGDLmGYosG-R3d4jGJz1vW_/view)).

| Code | Camera | Channel | Views grayed out | Features listed as not available |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C21A109 | Rear | 3 | Rear, rear cross path, free floating | **RAEB**, Full Automatic Park Assist, Personal Park Assist, Remote Parking Assist |
| C21A209 | Front | 4 | Front, front cross path, free floating | The three park features |
| C21A309 | Left (title wrongly says front) | 2 | Left side, left curb, left flank 3D, free floating | The three park features |
| C21A409 | Right | 1 | Right side, right curb, right flank 3D, free floating | The three park features |

Four readings of this data shape the trial. **The fastest a code can set is about 0.5 to 0.6 s**, so a disturbance shorter than 500 ms leaves no code, and a clean scan cannot prove an error-free link. **CRC is the criterion a marginal tap hits first**, and with no stated threshold it can fire long after the link looked stable. **Healing and aging are undocumented**: the reset and age fields are blank for these codes. Other ADAS codes that state a rule heal when the condition clears and age out after 40 drive cycles, but the rear and front entries list "Restart the system" as a test step, so plan as if a camera code holds its feature loss until at least the next ignition cycle. And **the same code reports calibration faults**, so a code that appears after a camera was moved or re-seated is not necessarily a link fault. No community tool is known to calibrate the surround cameras, which is the main reason not to unbolt or re-aim any camera.

One more monitor sits downstream. The ICC head unit sets P1A0502, "ICC ADAS Box Communication error", for missing data or checksum errors on the video link from the controller; it starts 120 s after ignition on and checks every 20 s, with the effect "Camera cannot work" ([dtc.json](https://fisker-ocean-archive.preetham-981.workers.dev/data/dtc.json)). A surround tap does not touch that link, but a test shorter than about two and a half minutes cannot see this code, so every hold time must exceed it. The controller also drops all video below 8.5 V for 10 s (U15B616), so a weak 12 V battery during a long ignition-on test produces faults that look like tap faults.

By the owner manual, only Rear View is available at speed (0 to 130 mph); the front wide, cross-traffic and 3D views stop above 10 mph, park assist works below 12 mph, and RAEB works at 0 to 10 mph in reverse ([Exterior Cameras](https://fisker-ocean-manual.pages.dev/manual/driving-and-operating/cameras/exterior-cameras/); [Automatic Emergency Braking](https://fisker-ocean-manual.pages.dev/manual/advanced-driver-assistance-systems-adas/safety-features/automatic-emergency-braking/)). So the ranking by consequence is rear first (RAEB and the reversing image that FMVSS 111 concerns), then front, then the two sides. No source shows a side camera feeding a driving-speed function, since blind-spot functions have their own corner-radar codes. That is absence of evidence, not proof. A fault on any link still raises a telltale, a chime and a pop-up, which distract a driver at any speed.

### Watching live on CAN

The controller publishes its surround-view and feature state on frames that both the comma (on bus 2, the ADAS side of the harness) and a head unit on IBUS2 can read. There is **no per-camera status signal on CAN**; the DTC is the only documented per-camera identity. The signals below come from the January 2023 CAN matrix as captured in the community DBC files ([fisker_ocean_ibus.dbc](https://github.com/benkayuk/oceanfront-view/blob/d5b1de09d095504adebdbc0c3b6d76d3463ae020/dbc/fisker_ocean_ibus.dbc); local copy `/Users/hiremanirmss/Fisker/oceanscape/dbc/fisker_ocean_adas_world.dbc`).

| Frame | Signal | Trouble value | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x316 | `ADASDC_SVS_AvailableSts` | 1 = system_fault (2 = too high speed is normal) | 50 ms |
| 0x316 | Ten view-button states, for example `LeftFlnk3DBttnShw` | 2 = show, fault, not press-able | 50 ms |
| 0x317 | `ADAS_SysFltTyp`, `ADAS_IDS_WarnTxtReq` | 8 = Surround_view_system_fault | 50 ms |
| 0x317 | `ADAS_SysFltWarnReq` | 1 or 2 = fault telltale requested | 50 ms |
| 0x311 | `ADAS_HiPrioDegradationSts` | 6 rear AEB disabled, 7 autopark disabled, 8 park assist disabled | 20 ms |
| 0x311 | `ADAS_LoPrioDegradationSts` | 6 park assist warnings disabled, 8 wheel scratch prevention disabled | 20 ms |
| 0x313 | `AEB_ReAEB_Sts` | 6 = error, or a degraded value | 20 ms |
| 0x31A | `ADAS_RAEBAvl` | 1 = not available | 50 ms |
| 0x314 | `ADAS_APASts`, `ADAS_WSPPASts` | 9 = fault; 4 = off, fault | 20 ms |
| 0x342 | `ADAS_Sts_RAP`, `ADAS_Sts_TP` | 4 = fault; 5 = fault | 50 ms |
| 0x481, 0x482 | One bit per internal fault | Any new bit | 100 ms |

**This fault signature is a prediction, not an observation.** It is built by matching the documented feature losses to the value tables; no capture of these signals during a real camera fault exists. The button-state mapping to a camera is a guess from signal names. `ADAS_RAEBAvl` and `AEB_ReAEB_Sts` probably change with gear and speed in normal use, so they mean something only against a baseline recorded in the same gear and speed. Values 3 to 13 of `ADASDC_SVS_AvailableSts` are undocumented and could include door-open or mirror-folded states, which the manual lists as surround-view limitations ([2D/3D Surround View System](https://fisker-ocean-manual.pages.dev/manual/advanced-driver-assistance-systems-adas/parking-features/2d-3d-surround-view-system/)).

For this repository the practical gaps are small. The port's fingerprints already include 0x313, 0x314, 0x316, 0x317, 0x31A, 0x481 and 0x482 on the ADAS bus (`opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/fisker/fingerprints.py`), and 0x311 is parsed live. But the DBC the port loads (`opendbc/dbc/fisker_ocean_adas.dbc`) has no 0x316, 0x342, 0x481 or 0x482 and lacks the two degradation signals in 0x311, and `carstate.py` reads none of the surround, RAEB, park or system-fault signals. Openpilot records raw CAN on all buses, so existing route logs should decode offline against the wider DBC, though no log was opened to confirm it. A live watcher needs only detection within one frame period after the controller changes state, well inside one second of a code maturing. One confound must be controlled: the comma already rewrites `ICC_0x35B` toward the controller and has caused ADAS codes when settings frames were disturbed, so the baseline code list may not be empty, and the comma's state must be identical before and after any tap test.

### Reading and clearing codes

Plain UDS over the OBD port reads and clears codes with no security access in the open-source tool. **Fiskeretta** (free, MIT) uses a Vgate vLinker FD+ adapter, lists the ADAS controller at request 0x7A6 and response 0x7AE and the ICC at 0x781/0x789 with a second ICC entry at 0x7B1/0x7B9, reads with service 0x19 (status mask, freeze frame, extended data) and clears with 0x14, allowing for slow replies because "radars/cameras can take seconds to wipe their fault memory" ([Fiskeretta](https://github.com/Fiskeretta/fiskeretta-diagnostics)). The car must be on and parked. Its repository contradicts itself (one file still lists ADAS as not yet reached), and no first-hand report shows a camera code read or cleared at 0x7A6 on current software, so prove the read in the baseline stage. **Freesker** is the paid alternative at EUR 99 (Lite) or EUR 478 (Pro), tied to one VIN; it advertises front-camera calibration but not surround-view calibration ([Freesker](https://freesker.com/)). Mainstream tools are reported not to carry the model ([diag.net](https://diag.net/discussion/szbjhkrp1cwqukksdzwiqipeoq)). The read that matters keeps the status byte: bit 0x01 says the fault is failing now, bit 0x08 that a code was confirmed and stored, and bit 0x04 that it is pending. A tool that shows only "active codes" hides whether a fault healed.

## The tap yields raw sensor data, so the ISP is half the project

If the best-guess parts are right, the camera link carries the sensor's own CSI-2 packets, and a silent deserializer forwards them unchanged. The expected stream is **RAW12 Bayer (CSI-2 data type 0x2C), 1920 x 1080 or 2048 x 1280, likely 30 fps, with HDR data companded from about 20 bits to 12**. That expectation rests on the sibling AR0231AT's format list and normal practice, not on an Ocean measurement; the real size and data type come from the deserializer's line registers and the packet headers. At 2048 x 1280 x 12 bit x 30 fps the payload is about 0.94 Gbps, which fits a two-lane CSI-2 output at 1 Gbps per lane only at 30 fps. Nothing on a "UB" camera link encrypts the video; the protections are integrity checks (parity, CRC), which matter to someone injecting video, not to a listener.

Turning that data into a picture takes, in order: black-level subtraction, decompanding with the sensor's piecewise-linear knee table, defective-pixel correction, lens-shading correction, white balance, demosaic, a colour-correction matrix, tone mapping to 8 bits, gamma, and fisheye undistortion. **The tap cannot read the values these steps need.** The knee points, black level, Bayer order and the per-frame exposure and gain are set over I2C on the back channel, which a silent tap does not see, and the AR0233AT's full datasheet and developer guide are under onsemi NDA. The workable substitutes are the sensor's embedded-data rows if Magna enabled them (a separate data type, 0x12), knee points estimated from histogram breaks, and a one-time checkerboard calibration through the tap for the lens. Because auto-exposure runs in the controller and the tap sees only its results, a fixed tone curve will look poor from day to night; white balance and tone mapping must be re-derived from each image.

Open ISP code covers part of this. libcamera's CPU software ISP does debayering, auto white balance, gain and gamma, but no source shows it decompanding HDR for this sensor, so a custom first stage is needed ([FOSDEM 2026 slides](https://fosdem.org/2026/events/attachments/TKSK3G-libcamera-softisp/slides/263009/libcamera_r0fvfbu.pdf)). A short custom pipeline (lookup-table decompand, OpenCV demosaic, a local tone map, a fisheye remap) on a GPU-capable board is a realistic estimate for one camera at 30 fps; four cameras is four times the load and was not sized. The host should have a native CSI-2 receiver that hands RAW12 frames to user space, such as a Jetson-class or RK3588-class board, wired to the deserializer's output with a fixed-format source description. Neither board's RAW12 capture path nor its price was verified. The mainline Linux `ds90ub960` driver assumes it can reach the serializer over the control channel, so a sniffer needs a patched or user-space configuration. A UVC bridge is the wrong shape for this job, since it would have to carry RAW12 in a non-standard format.

The result differs from what the car shows. The centre screen displays the controller's rendered, stitched, overlaid view; the tap gives four separate fisheye images with none of that work done. Matching the OEM picture is a software project of its own.

## A staged trial runs bench, baseline, coupler, receiver, then road

No source describes a tested tap on this car, so this procedure is a proposal built from the documented timings. Its logic is to change one thing at a time and to hold each state longer than the slowest monitor.

Two decisions come before any purchase. **Decide whether a single link can be tapped at all.** A quad coupler at `C003B*4F` passes all four links, including the rear one, through the added device. A single-link tap needs either a single-channel coupler at a side camera's door joint or a quad breakout that leaves three channels as straight pass-through, and no confirmed product exists for either at this connector. If the only practical device is the CPL0710, the trial is a four-link trial with RAEB at stake from the first ignition, and the bench bar rises to match. **Fix the comma's state for the whole trial**: either unplugged with the factory connection restored, or connected with one unchanged build.

| Stage | Action | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Bench, salvage only | Read chip markings on a salvage controller and camera. Measure DC on the coax. Measure the coupler's main-path insertion and return loss to 3 GHz with the tap port terminated, open, and with the receiver powered, unpowered, unplugged and shorted. Scope the receiver's input pin to prove it is silent from power-up. If a camera and controller will link on the bench, run for hours across temperature. | Family is FPD-Link III "UB". Main-path loss is a fraction of a dB with no resonance below 2.1 GHz. Main path is unchanged by every receiver state. Receiver never transmits. |
| 2. Car baseline, nothing added | Parked, level, doors, hood and liftgate closed, mirrors unfolded, 12 V supported above 9 V. Read all modules with status bytes (ADAS and both ICC addresses) and save; clear nothing. Record the CAN frames in the table above for at least 5 minutes in P, then R and D with the brake held, cycling each view. Photograph each view. Repeat on a second, colder day. | A stable, saved baseline of codes, signals and images. |
| 3. Connector handling | Ignition off, car asleep. Disconnect and reconnect `C003B*4F` once with no coupler. Repeat the stage 2 reads. | No change from baseline. This separates handling faults from coupler faults. |
| 4. Coupler in line, tap port terminated | Fit with the car asleep. Watch CAN for the first 10 s after ignition on. Hold at least 10 minutes, exercise every view in P, R and D. Read codes. Do at least 5 ignition cycles, some after a full sleep. | No new code, no signal change, no image change. |
| 5. Receiver connected | Repeat stage 4 with the receiver powered. Then power it off, then unplug it, with the car on. | Main path does not react to any receiver state. |
| 6. Private ground, under 10 mph | Only after stages 4 and 5 are clean on several days. A second person watches the live signals. Use park features; include bumps and full steering lock. Read codes after. | Clean. |
| 7. Road use | A live indicator shows `ADAS_SysFltTyp`, `ADASDC_SVS_AvailableSts` and the degradation signals. Read codes after each of the first several drives, then weekly; repeat the full check at the first cold spell and the first hot spell. | Clean across seasons. |

The 10-minute hold in stage 4 covers the 120 s start and many 20 s cycles of P1A0502 and gives the unthresholded CRC criterion time to act; it is a judgement, not a guarantee. Cold matters because owner complaints tie camera failures to low temperatures. Decode logs on the Mac, not on the comma.

**Stop conditions.** Remove the tap and restore the factory connection on any of these: a new stored, pending or currently failing code in ADAS or ICC, above all C21A109 to C21A409, P1A0502, U15B616 or U15B896; any trouble value in the CAN table that was absent from the baseline in the same gear and speed; a new bit in 0x481 or 0x482; a grayed, frozen, flickering, tearing or noisy feed, or a view that opens later than in the baseline, even with no code; any new driver-display notification, telltale or chime; and, as an immediate stop regardless of codes, a reversing image that appears late or not at all. If 12 V falls below 9 V the result is uninterpretable, so stop and repeat. A stop condition met twice on separate attempts ends the trial for that coupler.

**Rollback.** With the ignition off and the car asleep, remove the coupler, reconnect the factory plug and confirm that it latches. Switch on, wait at least 3 minutes, and check every view and the reversing image. Read codes and look at bit 0x01 first: if the code is no longer failing now, the condition has healed and only the stored record remains. Save the full report with freeze-frame data, then clear ADAS and ICC with service 0x14 and allow for a slow reply. Run two more ignition cycles and a short private-ground check, and confirm the code lists and CAN signals match the baseline. If a camera code stays "failing now" with the factory connection restored, stop: the remaining causes are a damaged or unseated connector, a damaged cable, or the calibration criteria, and the last needs FAST-level service, which OV Loop sells remotely at $150 to $250 per hour with a two-hour minimum.

## Eight unknowns stand between the bench and the car

The open questions fall into two groups: those a salvage unit answers cheaply, and those only the car or a vendor can answer.

A salvage controller and camera settle the first four. The deserializer and serializer part numbers decide the family, the receive mode and whether HDCP exists. The coax voltage and camera current decide the coupler variant and whether a coupler's DC path is adequate. The physical cavity order of `C003B*4F` decides which channel is which. And the mating header's part number decides whether any catalogue plug fits. One caution applies: a salvage controller probably gives no video without its vehicle partners on the network, so it is first a source of markings and measurements, and the bench link test may never run. The Fiskeretta data also lists the controller as FM2980140120**H** where the parts book says FM2980140120**B**, so hardware or software revisions exist and a salvage unit may differ from the car's.

The remaining four need outside input. TZ must supply S-parameters, the PoC current rating, connector gender and coding, a price, and the "coupler mode" settings; without measured loss the link margin cannot be judged. Receiver sensitivity at the coupled port is undocumented. Whether the camera codes latch, and whether community tools read and clear them at 0x7A6 on current software, is answerable only on the car. And whether any driving-speed function uses the surround cameras on software 2.x is not established.

The risks rank by cost. The worst case is a calibration-type code, or a damaged quad connector, that community tools cannot clear, which means a FAST session or a harness repair on a car with no manufacturer. Next is an intermittent CRC fault that removes RAEB and park features in cold weather, weeks after a clean trial. Third is a receiver that transmits on the camera link after a firmware fault. Fourth is the project succeeding electrically and stalling on the ISP for want of the sensor's NDA documents. Heat is a smaller, physical risk: capture hardware behind the same trim must not block the controller's airflow. The legal frame is unchanged: FMVSS 111 requires the rear image within 2.0 seconds of a backing event ([49 CFR 571.111](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.111)), the federal make-inoperative ban names businesses and not owners ([49 U.S.C. 30122](https://openjurist.org/49/usc/30122)), and neither point is legal advice.

## Recommendation: aftermarket cameras for the display, a gated bench study for the tap

The two routes answer different needs, so the choice turns on what the display is for.

| Criterion | Passive tap on the four OEM links | Four aftermarket cameras |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware cost | About $1,400 to $1,700 for the bench alone, plus coupler quote, host board and scope rental | About $250 to $500 |
| Image source | OEM sensors in factory positions: 2048 x 1280 HDR with LED flicker mitigation | 720p to 1080p generic cameras on brackets |
| Work after capture | Full custom ISP with NDA-gated sensor data | None; the modules output UVC video |
| Risk to the car | Camera codes, loss of park features and RAEB, possible uncleared calibration fault | No contact with OEM video; mounting must stay clear of factory sensors |
| Precedent | None public | No Ocean install found, but standard practice |
| Time to first picture | Months, with several unknowns that can end the project | Days to weeks |
| Latency | One deserializer plus the ISP; unmeasured | About 100 to 200 ms estimated; unmeasured |

**For a working third-party display, fit aftermarket cameras.** They deliver independent, always-on views at one-fifth the cost with no path to an ADAS fault. Their open questions (USB bandwidth on the head unit, mounting positions) are cheap to test with one module.

**Pursue the tap only as a research project, and only through gates.** The first gate costs a few hundred dollars: buy a salvage controller, and a camera if one can be found, read the markings, and measure the coax voltage. In parallel, ask TZ for the CPL0710's S-parameters, PoC rating, connector details, price and coupler-mode register notes. Stop if the chips are not TI "UB" FPD-Link III parts, if TZ will not sell or will not give loss data, or if no way exists to keep the rear link out of the tapped path at an acceptable bench margin. If those answers are good, buy the evaluation board and VNA and complete stage 1. Take hardware to the car only when the bench shows a silent receiver, a main path unaffected by every receiver state, and through-loss of a fraction of a dB. Start in the car with the baseline and connector-handling stages, which cost nothing and are worth doing regardless: they prove the diagnostic read at 0x7A6 and the CAN signature, and they show whether the comma has already left ADAS codes.

One piece of this work pays off under either route. Adding the surround-view, RAEB, park-assist and system-fault signals to the port's DBC and logging them gives a live ADAS health indicator that the car currently lacks on the comma side, and it produces the baseline any later tap trial needs.

## Conclusion

The research changed the shape of the problem more than its answer. The earlier picture was a tap behind the dash on links of unknown type; the documents now show a controller in the cargo area, one well-specified blue connector, a confirmed sensor, and a fault monitor described precisely enough to design a trial around. That precision cuts both ways. It makes a careful trial possible, and it shows that the monitor's most likely trigger for a marginal tap, the CRC check, is the one criterion with no documented threshold and no documented recovery.

The deeper finding is that the electrical tap is the smaller half. Even a perfect, fault-free tap delivers companded Bayer data whose decoding parameters sit behind an NDA, in a compartment far from the display. A project that begins as "borrow the car's cameras" ends as "build a camera pipeline for someone else's sensor", while aftermarket cameras deliver the picture directly. The tap earns its cost only if OEM sensor quality and factory mounting are the goal in themselves, and the salvage controller, at about $300, is the cheapest way to find out whether that goal is even reachable.
